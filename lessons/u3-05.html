Shaping trees for light, strength and fruit

An untrained, unpruned fruit tree becomes a tall, dense bush – fruits are few, small and out of reach, and pests hide inside. Training gives a young tree a strong frame; pruning keeps a bearing tree productive, healthy and easy to manage.

5.1 Training and pruning – definitions

TrainingPruning
Development of a desired shape and strong framework by cutting, bending, tying and supporting young plantsJudicious removal of plant parts – shoots, branches, spurs, roots – to improve productivity, quality and health
Done mainly in young, non-bearing plantsDone mainly in bearing plants
Aim – strong scaffold, balanced canopyAim – balance between vegetative growth and fruiting

Explained simply

Training is like shaping a child's habits while young; pruning is like a regular haircut for adults.

5.2 Objectives of training and pruning

  • Build a strong framework able to carry heavy fruit load.
  • Allow sunlight and air into the canopy – better fruit colour, sugar and fewer diseases.
  • Regulate crop load for regular bearing and quality.
  • Remove dead, diseased, broken, crossing and water shoots.
  • Keep tree height and spread convenient for spraying and harvesting.
  • Rejuvenate old, unproductive trees.

5.3 Principles of pruning

  • Know the bearing habit – fruits on current season's growth (grape, ber, guava, pomegranate), on one-year-old shoots (mango, citrus) or on spurs (apple).
  • Light pruning promotes fruiting; heavy pruning promotes vegetative growth.
  • Make clean cuts just above an outward-facing bud; do not leave stubs.
  • Protect large cuts with Bordeaux paste.
  • Prune at the right time – dormant season for deciduous trees, after harvest for evergreen trees.

Facts & amazing information

1

Grapes in Maharashtra are pruned twice a year – that is why fresh grapes come to market in January–April.

2

Rejuvenated old mango trees start bearing again within 2–3 years of heavy pruning.

3

At least 30 % sunlight penetration into the canopy is needed for good fruit colour.

4

Ber trees are pruned so heavily that they look like stumps in May, yet bear fully by winter.

Long-answer questions for ESE

Each question shows marks, Bloom's Taxonomy Level (BTL) and the course outcome it assesses.

  1. Differentiate training and pruning and analyse how the bearing habit of a fruit crop decides its pruning method. (10 marks · BTL-4 Analyse · CO3)

    Answer points: Definitions and table; bearing habits with examples; light vs heavy pruning; crop examples.

  2. Describe the central leader, open centre and modified leader systems with diagrams and evaluate their suitability for mango, pomegranate and apple. (10 marks · BTL-5 Evaluate · CO3)

    Answer points: Diagrams; merits and demerits; suitability with reasons.

  3. Explain the double-pruning system of grapes in Maharashtra and justify its timing in relation to climate and market. (10 marks · BTL-5 Evaluate · CO3)

    Answer points: Back pruning April – cane development; fruit pruning October – winter growth, harvest Jan–April; rest period; export market.

  4. Plan the rejuvenation of an old, unproductive mango orchard and explain the after-care required. (10 marks · BTL-6 Create · CO3)

    Answer points: Selection of trees; heading back at 3–4 m in Dec–Jan; Bordeaux paste; shoot thinning; nutrition; irrigation; pest care; results.

  5. Describe the types of pruning with examples and their effects on growth and fruiting. (5 marks · BTL-2 Understand · CO3)

    Answer points: Heading back, thinning out, root pruning, rejuvenation, summer and dormant pruning.
