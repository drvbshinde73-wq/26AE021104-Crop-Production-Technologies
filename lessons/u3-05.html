Shaping trees for light, strength and fruit

An unpruned fruit tree becomes a tall, dense bush – fruits are few, small and out of reach. Training gives the tree a strong frame; pruning keeps it productive.

5.1 Definitions

  • Training – developing a desired shape and strong framework of the tree by cutting, bending and tying young plants.
  • Pruning – judicious removal of plant parts (shoots, branches, roots) to improve productivity, quality and health of bearing trees.

Explained simply

Training is like shaping a child's habits while young; pruning is like a regular haircut for adults.

5.2 Objectives

  • Strong framework able to carry fruit load.
  • Admit sunlight and air into the canopy – better colour and fewer diseases.
  • Regulate crop load for regular bearing and quality fruit.
  • Remove dead, diseased, crossing and water shoots.
  • Keep tree height manageable for spraying and harvesting.

5.4 Types and methods of pruning

  • Heading back – cutting the terminal part of a shoot; encourages branching.
  • Thinning out – removing an entire shoot at its origin; opens the canopy.
  • Root pruning – to check excessive vigour and induce flowering.
  • Rejuvenation pruning – heavy cutting of old, unproductive mango or citrus trees to produce new canopy.
  • Dormant (winter) and summer pruning as per crop.
  • Seal large cuts with Bordeaux paste.

Facts & amazing information

1

Grapes in Maharashtra are pruned twice a year – that is why we get grapes in January–April.

2

Rejuvenated old mango trees start bearing again within 2–3 years of heavy pruning.

3

Sunlight penetration of at least 30 % into the canopy is needed for good fruit colour.

Possible ESE questions

Short-answer questions

  1. Define training. (2 marks)

    Answer points: Developing desired shape and strong framework of young plants by cutting, bending and tying.

  2. What is heading back? (2 marks)

    Answer points: Cutting the terminal portion of a shoot to promote branching.

  3. What is double pruning in grape? (2 marks)

    Answer points: Back pruning in April and fruit pruning in October.

Long-answer questions

  1. Differentiate training and pruning and state their objectives. (5 marks)

    Answer points: Definitions, stage of plant, aim, methods; objectives list.

  2. Describe the systems of training fruit trees. (10 marks)

    Answer points: Central leader, open centre, modified leader, trellis systems for vines, espalier; merits, crops, diagrams.

  3. Explain the types of pruning with examples of grape, pomegranate and mango. (10 marks)

    Answer points: Heading back, thinning out, root pruning, rejuvenation; crop-wise pruning schedules.
