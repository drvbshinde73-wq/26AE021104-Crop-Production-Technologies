Every drop, every root

Horticultural crops are high-value but thirsty – banana needs about 1,800–2,200 mm of water a year. Supplying the right amount of water at the right place (the root zone) and covering the soil with mulch can save half the water and raise yield and quality.

26.1 Importance of water in horticulture

  • Fruits and vegetables contain 80–95 % water; water stress reduces size, juiciness and quality.
  • Critical stages – flowering, fruit set and fruit development; in mango and pomegranate, controlled stress before flowering induces flowering.
  • Over-irrigation causes root rot, nutrient leaching, cracking (pomegranate, tomato) and poor quality.

Explained simply

Drip irrigation works like an IV drip in a hospital – small, steady doses delivered right where they are needed.

26.3 Drip irrigation and fertigation

  • Saves 40–60 % water and 25–30 % fertilizer; increases yield by 20–50 %; fewer weeds and diseases; suits sloping land and saline water.
  • Fertigation – applying water-soluble fertilizers (19:19:19, urea, potassium nitrate, mono-ammonium phosphate) through drip in small weekly doses matched to crop demand.
  • Limitations: high initial cost (subsidy under PMKSY – ‘Per Drop More Crop’), clogging of drippers – needs acid and chlorine treatment, rodent damage.

Key point

Water requirement (L/day/plant) = Pan evaporation (mm) × Pan coefficient × Crop coefficient (Kc) × Area (m²) × Wetted fraction

26.5 Mulching

Mulching is the covering of the soil surface around plants with organic or synthetic materials to conserve moisture, control weeds and moderate soil temperature.

TypeMaterialsAdvantages
Organic mulchStraw, sugarcane trash, dry leaves, sawdust, coir pith, grassAdds organic matter, cools soil, cheap
Plastic mulch – blackLLDPE film 25–50 µmWeed control, moisture saving
Silver-on-blackReflective upper surfaceRepels aphids, thrips and whitefly; cooler soil
TransparentClear filmSoil solarisation against soil-borne pathogens and weeds
Live mulch / cover cropsCowpea, horse gramProtects soil, fixes N
  • Benefits: saves 25–50 % soil moisture, controls weeds, reduces evaporation, prevents erosion and soil splash, keeps fruits clean, improves yield and earliness.
  • Limitations: cost and disposal of plastic; organic mulch may harbour termites and rodents.

Long-answer questions for ESE

  1. Compare the surface, sprinkler and micro-irrigation methods used in horticultural crops and recommend the most suitable method for banana, leafy vegetables and old mango orchards. (10 marks · BTL-5 Evaluate · CO3)

    Answer points: Methods with efficiency; suitability; recommendations with reasons.

  2. Describe the components of a drip-irrigation system with a neat sketch and analyse its advantages and limitations. (10 marks · BTL-4 Analyse · CO3)

    Answer points: Pump, filters, venturi, mains, sub-mains, laterals, drippers; advantages; limitations; maintenance.

  3. Calculate the daily water requirement of a pomegranate plant at 4.5 × 3 m spacing when pan evaporation is 8 mm, pan coefficient 0.7, Kc 0.8 and wetted fraction 0.5. (5 marks · BTL-3 Apply · CO3)

    Answer points: 8 × 0.7 × 0.8 × 13.5 × 0.5 = 30.2 L/plant/day; explanation of terms.

  4. Explain fertigation and design a weekly fertigation schedule for drip-irrigated banana. (10 marks · BTL-6 Create · CO3)

    Answer points: Principle; fertilizers; total dose 200:40:200 g NPK/plant split by growth stage; schedule table; precautions.

  5. Evaluate the role of organic and plastic mulches in moisture conservation, weed control and pest management in vegetable crops. (10 marks · BTL-5 Evaluate · CO3)

    Answer points: Types; mechanisms; silver mulch and pests; solarisation; yields; limitations and disposal.
