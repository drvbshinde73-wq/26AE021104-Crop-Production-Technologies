Sorting 100 crops into sensible boxes

India grows well over a hundred field crops. A student cannot learn each one separately – so agronomists classify them. Once you know that chickpea is a rabi pulse, a C3 legume and a long-day plant, you can already predict its season, its need for nitrogen, its response to temperature and even its place in a crop rotation. Classification is a short-cut to understanding.

12.1 Why classify field crops?

  • To study a very large number of crops systematically.

  • To group crops with similar requirements of climate, soil, water and management.

  • To plan cropping systems and rotations (e.g. follow a cereal with a legume).

  • To decide sowing season, spacing and input needs quickly.

  • To simplify research, extension, statistics and marketing.

Explained simply

A library does not stack books randomly; it arranges them by subject, author and language. Crops are classified in the same way – by season, use, life span, botanical family, photoperiod, CO2 fixation and special purpose. One crop can sit in several boxes at once: maize is a kharif crop, a cereal, an annual, a C4 plant and a day-neutral plant.

12.2 Classification based on season

SeasonSowing – harvestWeather during growthExamples
Kharif (monsoon)June–July → Sept–OctHot, humid, rainy; long daysRice, maize, kharif jowar, bajra, soybean, groundnut, cotton, tur, moong, urad
Rabi (winter)Oct–Nov → Feb–AprilCool, dry growth; warm ripening; short daysWheat, chickpea, rabi jowar, safflower, mustard, linseed, lentil
Zaid (summer)Feb–March → May–JuneHot, dry, long days; irrigatedSummer groundnut, moong, sunflower, watermelon, cucumber, fodder maize

Maharashtra example

On the deep black soils of Solapur and Ahilyanagar, farmers leave the land fallow in kharif and grow rabi jowar (e.g. Maldandi 35-1) on stored soil moisture – a classic rabi crop of Maharashtra. In Vidarbha, soybean or cotton is grown in kharif and chickpea follows in rabi on residual moisture.

12.3 Classification based on economic use (agronomic classification)

This is the most widely used classification in agronomy.

GroupDefinitionExamples
1. CerealsCultivated grasses (family Poaceae) grown for edible starchy grainRice, wheat, maize, barley, oats
2. MilletsSmall-grained cereals, hardy, grown on poor soils with low rainfallMajor: sorghum (jowar), pearl millet (bajra), finger millet (ragi); minor: foxtail, kodo, little, barnyard, proso millet
3. Pulses (grain legumes)Legumes (Fabaceae) grown for protein-rich seed; fix atmospheric NChickpea, pigeonpea (tur), green gram, black gram, lentil, cowpea, field pea
4. OilseedsSeeds rich in oilGroundnut, soybean, mustard, sunflower, safflower, sesame, linseed, niger, castor
5. Fibre cropsGrown for fibreSeed fibre: cotton; bast/stem fibre: jute, mesta, sunhemp; leaf fibre: sisal
6. Sugar cropsGrown for sugarSugarcane, sugar beet
7. Starch / tuber cropsStore starch in underground organsPotato, sweet potato, tapioca (cassava)
8. Forage cropsFed to livestock as green fodder, hay or silageCereal: fodder maize, sorghum, oats; legume: lucerne, berseem, cowpea; grasses: hybrid napier
9. Spices and condimentsUsed to flavour foodChilli, turmeric, coriander, cumin, fenugreek, ginger
10. Medicinal and aromaticGrown for drugs or essential oilsAshwagandha, isabgol, menthol mint, lemongrass, tulsi
11. Beverages and narcoticsStimulating drinks or narcoticsTea, coffee (plantation); tobacco
12. Green-manure cropsGrown and ploughed in to enrich soilSunhemp, dhaincha (Sesbania), glyricidia (green-leaf manure)

Watch out

Soybean and groundnut are legumes but are classified as oilseeds, not pulses, because they are grown mainly for oil. Likewise, a pulse grown for fodder (cowpea) becomes a forage crop. The use decides the agronomic group.

12.5 Classification based on photoperiod (day length)

Photoperiodism is the response of plants to the relative length of day and night, mainly for flowering (Garner and Allard, 1920).

GroupFlowers whenExamples
Short-day plants (long-night)Day length is shorter than a critical valueRice, soybean, sorghum, cotton, tobacco, sugarcane, maize (most tropical types)
Long-day plantsDay length is longer than a critical valueWheat, barley, oats, chickpea, mustard, lentil, potato
Day-neutral plantsFlowering not controlled by day lengthSunflower, tomato, cucumber, many modern hybrids

Why it matters

A short-day soybean sown too late in the season (shorter days) flowers too early on a small plant and yields poorly. That is why sowing date is a photoperiod decision, not only a rainfall decision.

Exam tip: Rice and wheat are C3 cereals, while maize, sorghum, bajra and sugarcane are C4. C4 crops have high productivity and water-use efficiency in hot climates.

Special-purpose cropMeaning

Try it yourself

Make a table of ten crops grown in your district. For each crop fill in: season, agronomic group, life span, photoperiod response and C3/C4. You will notice that most kharif cereals are C4 short-day plants, while most rabi crops are C3 long-day plants – can you explain why?

Facts & amazing information

1

The United Nations declared 2023 the International Year of Millets on India's proposal; millets are now promoted as ‘Shree Anna’.

2

Sugarcane is one of the most efficient C4 plants – it can produce over 100 t of cane per hectare in a year.

3

Pulses add about 30–150 kg N/ha to the soil through symbiotic nitrogen fixation with Rhizobium.

4

Rice is a short-day plant, but many modern Indian rice varieties are photo-insensitive, so they can be grown in more than one season.

5

India is the largest producer and consumer of pulses in the world, yet still imports pulses to meet demand.

6

Wheat originated in the Fertile Crescent of West Asia, maize in Mexico, and rice in South-East Asia/India – classification by origin explains their climate needs.

Long-answer questions for ESE

  1. Explain the classification of crops based on photoperiod with examples. (5 marks · BTL-2 Understand · CO2)

    Answer points: Short-day, long-day, day-neutral; definition, critical day length, examples, practical significance in sowing date.

  2. Give the agronomic classification of field crops with suitable examples. (7 marks · BTL-2 Understand · CO2)

    Answer points: Cereals, millets, pulses, oilseeds, fibre, sugar, starch, forage, spices, medicinal/aromatic, beverages/narcotics, green manures with examples.

  3. Discuss the special-purpose classification of crops. Design a cropping plan for a 2-ha rainfed farm in Marathwada that uses catch, cover, trap and contingency crops. (8 marks · BTL-3 Apply · CO2)

    Answer points: Definitions; plan – soybean + tur intercrop, marigold trap crop, horse gram cover, contingency crops (sunflower, bajra) for late monsoon; justification.

  4. Evaluate the role of millets as ‘climate-smart crops’ for Indian dryland agriculture. Classify millets and compare their agronomic and nutritional advantages with those of rice and wheat. (8 marks · BTL-5 Evaluate · CO2)

    Answer points: Major and minor millets; C4, drought tolerance, short duration, low input; nutrient density (Fe, Ca, fibre); limitations – yield, processing, market; policy (Shree Anna).

  5. “A single crop can belong to several classes simultaneously.” Illustrate this statement using maize, soybean and chickpea, and explain how each class guides a different management decision. (10 marks · BTL-5 Evaluate · CO2)

    Answer points: Season, use, family, photoperiod, CO2 pathway, root system; decisions – sowing date, nutrient (N) management, rotation, water, spacing.

  6. Critically compare C3, C4 and CAM pathways of CO2 fixation. Explain why C4 crops dominate the hot semi-arid regions of India, and predict how rising temperature and CO2 may change this advantage. (12 marks · BTL-5 Evaluate · CO2)

    Answer points: Biochemistry, enzymes, anatomy, photorespiration, temperature optima, WUE; C4 advantage at high temperature and light; elevated CO2 favours C3 (reduced photorespiration) but heat favours C4; implications.

Key words

Kharif
monsoon season crops sown in June–July
Rabi
winter season crops sown in October–November
Zaid
short summer season crops between rabi and kharif
Photoperiodism
response of plants to day length
C4 plant
plant whose first CO2 product is a 4-carbon acid; high efficiency in heat
Catch crop
short crop fitted between two main crops
Trap crop
crop grown to attract pests away from the main crop
