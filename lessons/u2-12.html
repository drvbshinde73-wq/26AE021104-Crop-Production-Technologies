Sorting crops like a librarian sorts books

India grows more than 100 field crops. To study, plan and manage them, agronomists group crops by their use, season, botany, life span, photosynthesis, day-length response and root system – just as a librarian sorts books by subject. One crop belongs to several groups at once: maize is a cereal, a kharif and rabi crop, a C4 plant, a day-neutral annual and an exhaustive crop.

12.1 Why classify field crops?

  • To plan crop rotations, intercropping and cropping systems.
  • To choose the right season, management and inputs for groups with similar needs.
  • To organise research, statistics, extension and policy (e.g. MSP, missions).
  • To understand crop adaptation to climate and soil.

Explained simply

C4 crops have a ‘turbo-charger’ that concentrates CO2 inside the leaf, so they keep photosynthesising efficiently in hot, bright and dry weather – that is why maize and sugarcane yield so much biomass.

Facts & amazing information

Maize is called the ‘queen of cereals’ because of its very high genetic yield potential.

2

3

Pulses fix about 30–150 kg N/ha through Rhizobium nodules.

4

Long-answer questions for ESE

  1. Classify field crops on the basis of economic use and evaluate the usefulness of this classification for planning cropping systems. (10 marks · BTL-5 Evaluate · CO2)

    Answer points: Twelve groups with examples; usefulness – rotation (cereal–legume), market planning, input needs; limitations (multi-purpose crops).

  2. Differentiate C3 and C4 crops in terms of anatomy, biochemistry and agronomic performance, and analyse their response to rising temperature and CO2. (10 marks · BTL-4 Analyse · CO2)

    Answer points: 3-PGA vs OAA; Kranz anatomy; photorespiration; WUE, NUE; optimum temperature; C3 benefit more from CO2, C4 tolerate heat; examples.

  3. Explain photoperiodic classification of crops and apply it to decide sowing dates of soybean and wheat. (5 marks · BTL-3 Apply · CO2)

    Answer points: Short-day, long-day, day-neutral; critical photoperiod; soybean flowering accelerated by short days – timely June sowing; wheat needs long days – November sowing; consequences of late sowing.

  4. Examine the role of catch, cover, trap and smother crops in sustainable crop production with suitable Indian examples. (10 marks · BTL-4 Analyse · CO2)

    Answer points: Definitions; examples; soil protection, weed suppression, pest management, extra income; integration in cropping systems.

  5. Discuss the different bases of classification of field crops and show how one crop such as maize fits into several groups. (5 marks · BTL-2 Understand · CO2)

    Answer points: Economic use, season, botany, life span, photosynthesis, photoperiod, root depth, water regime, special purpose; maize as cereal, kharif/rabi, C4, day-neutral, exhaustive.
