Protein, oil and fibre from the black soils

Soybean, chickpea and cotton dominate the black soils of Vidarbha and Marathwada. Soybean and chickpea supply protein and oil while enriching the soil with nitrogen; cotton supplies fibre to India's textile industry. Their success depends on moisture conservation, timely sowing and pest management.

18.1 Soybean (Glycine max (L.) Merr.)

PracticeRecommendation
Climate and soilWarm humid kharif; 700–1,000 mm rain; medium-to-deep well-drained black soils; waterlogging harmful
VarietiesJS-335, JS-9560, Phule Sangam (KDS-726), MACS-1188, MAUS-71, MAUS-158, Phule Kimaya
SowingAfter 75–100 mm cumulative rain (mid-June to mid-July); BBF or ridge and furrow
Seed rate and spacing65–75 kg/ha; 45 × 5 cm (30 cm on BBF); germination test before sowing
Seed treatmentThiram + carbendazim (2 : 1) 3 g/kg, then Bradyrhizobium japonicum + PSB 25 g/kg
Nutrients50 : 75 : 45 kg N : P₂O₅ : K₂O/ha + sulphur 20 kg/ha; FYM 5 t/ha
WeedsCritical period first 30–45 days; imazethapyr post-emergence or hand weeding
ProtectionGirdle beetle, semilooper, tobacco caterpillar, stem fly; yellow mosaic virus, rust, charcoal rot
Harvest and yieldWhen 95 % pods turn brown; 25–30 q/ha

18.2 Chickpea (Cicer arietinum L.)

PracticeRecommendation
Climate and soilCool dry rabi; residual moisture of medium-black soils; frost and excess moisture harmful
Types and varietiesDesi – Vijay, Digvijay, Phule Vikram (machine-harvestable), JAKI-9218; kabuli – Kripa, Virat
SowingRainfed: September end–October on conserved moisture; irrigated: October–November
Seed rate and spacingDesi 65–75 kg/ha, kabuli 100 kg/ha; 30 × 10 cm
Seed treatmentThiram + carbendazim, then Trichoderma 5 g/kg, Rhizobium + PSB 25 g/kg
Nutrients25 : 50 : 0 kg N : P₂O₅ : K₂O/ha + sulphur 20 kg/ha
NippingRemoval of shoot tips at 30–40 days to increase branching
WaterLight irrigations at branching and pod filling; avoid excess
ProtectionPod borer (Helicoverpa armigera) – pheromone traps, bird perches, HaNPV, need-based sprays; Fusarium wilt – resistant varieties
Harvest and yieldWhen plants and pods turn yellow-brown; 20–25 q/ha

18.3 Cotton (Gossypium hirsutum L.)

PracticeRecommendation
Climate and soilWarm, frost-free 180–200 days; 500–1,000 mm rain; deep black soils (Vertisols)
TypesBt hybrids (Bollgard II), straight varieties (PKV Rajat, NH-615), desi cotton (G. arboreum) for low-input areas
SowingRainfed: with monsoon (75–100 mm rain); irrigated pre-monsoon: late May
Spacing and seedBt hybrids 90 × 60 or 120 × 45 cm, ≈ 2–2.5 kg seed/ha; high-density planting system (HDPS) 90 × 15 cm with compact varieties
RefugeNon-Bt refuge rows (or refuge-in-bag) to delay pink-bollworm resistance
NutrientsBt irrigated 120 : 60 : 60, rainfed 60 : 30 : 30 kg/ha; N in splits; foliar 2 % urea/DAP at flowering; MgSO₄ against leaf reddening
WaterCritical stages – square formation, flowering and boll development; drip with fertigation
ProtectionPink bollworm, whitefly, jassids, thrips; root rot, bacterial blight – pheromone traps, destruction of rosette flowers, crop termination by December, stubble destruction
Harvest and yieldPicking in 3–4 rounds of fully opened bolls; 20–25 q/ha seed cotton (irrigated)

Key point

Ending the cotton crop by December and destroying stubble breaks the life cycle of pink bollworm – the cheapest and most effective control.

Facts & amazing information

1

Cotton is a perennial shrub by nature, but agronomy grows it as an annual crop.

2

Soybean seed contains about 40 % protein and 20 % oil – the richest protein source among field crops.

3

Chickpea is India's most important pulse, contributing about half of total pulse production.

4

Phule Vikram chickpea grows tall and upright so it can be harvested by combine.

Long-answer questions for ESE

  1. Design a soybean production plan for a rainfed medium-black soil, with special emphasis on broad bed and furrow sowing and seed treatment. (10 marks · BTL-6 Create · CO2)

    Answer points: Variety, sowing window, BBF dimensions and benefits, seed rate, FIR treatment, nutrients incl. S, weed control, IPM, harvest.

  2. Analyse the integrated management of pod borer in chickpea. (10 marks · BTL-4 Analyse · CO2)

    Answer points: Biology; ETL; pheromone traps, bird perches, intercrop/trap crops, HaNPV, NSKE, need-based insecticides; timing.

  3. Critically evaluate refuge requirement, high-density planting and crop termination as strategies for sustainable Bt cotton production. (10 marks · BTL-5 Evaluate · CO2)

    Answer points: Resistance evolution; refuge principle; HDPS benefits for rainfed areas; termination and stubble destruction; evidence.

  4. Compare the nutrient management of soybean, chickpea and cotton and explain why legumes need less nitrogen. (10 marks · BTL-4 Analyse · CO2)

    Answer points: Doses; biological N fixation; S needs; foliar nutrition in cotton; residual benefit.

  5. Describe the importance of nipping in chickpea and sulphur nutrition in oilseeds. (5 marks · BTL-2 Understand · CO2)

    Answer points: Apical dominance, branching, pods; S in oil and protein synthesis; sources.
