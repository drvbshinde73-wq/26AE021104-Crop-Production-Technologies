Seven crops that feed and clothe India

Rice and wheat fill our plates, maize feeds poultry and industry, sugarcane gives sugar and ethanol, soybean and chickpea give protein and oil, and cotton clothes us. Knowing their production package – season, variety, seed, spacing, nutrients, water and protection – is the core of agronomy.

6.3 Cereals – rice, wheat and maize

  • Rice: clayey, water-retentive soils; puddling reduces percolation; raise nursery (1/10 ha for 1 ha), transplant 21–25-day seedlings; keep 2–5 cm water; give half N at transplanting and the rest at tillering and panicle initiation; SRI saves water and seed. Harvest when 80 % grains turn golden.
  • Wheat: well-drained loam to clay loam; fine seedbed; seed treatment with carbendazim and Azotobacter; 6 irrigations at CRI, tillering, jointing, flowering, milk and dough stages (CRI is the most critical); half N basal, half at first irrigation.
  • Maize: well-drained fertile soils, no waterlogging; ridge sowing; atrazine pre-emergence for weeds; earthing up at 30–35 DAS; monitor fall armyworm and use whorl application of approved insecticide; harvest when husk turns dry and black layer forms.

6.4 Sugarcane

  • Deep, well-drained medium-to-heavy soils; deep ploughing and 120–150 cm furrows.
  • Use healthy 9–10-month-old seed cane; treat setts with carbendazim (0.1 %) and Acetobacter + PSB; hot-water treatment against grassy shoot.
  • Bud-chip / single-bud seedlings from protrays save seed and give uniform stand.
  • Apply N in splits; trash mulching; drip irrigation saves 40–50 % water and raises yield; earthing up at 4–5 months.
  • Harvest at maturity (brix 18–20, hand refractometer); ratoon management – stubble shaving, gap filling, extra N.

6.5 Pulses and oilseed – chickpea and soybean

  • Soybean: medium-to-deep well-drained soils; sow on BBF after adequate rain; seed treatment with thiram + Rhizobium + PSB; weed-free for the first 45 days; protect against girdle beetle and defoliators; harvest when 95 % pods turn brown.
  • Chickpea: medium-black soils with residual moisture; do not over-irrigate; nipping at 30–40 DAS increases branching; pheromone traps and NPV against Helicoverpa; wilt-resistant varieties; harvest when plants and pods turn yellow-brown.

6.6 Cotton

  • Deep black soils (Vertisols) of Vidarbha and Marathwada; good drainage.
  • Bt hybrids with 20 % refuge (non-Bt) to delay resistance; high-density planting system (HDPS) for rainfed areas.
  • Split N application; foliar 2 % DAP / urea at flowering and boll development; MgSO4 against leaf reddening.
  • Pink bollworm management: timely sowing, pheromone traps, destroy rosette flowers, avoid extending the crop beyond December.
  • Picking in 3–4 rounds when bolls burst fully; keep kapas clean and dry.

Facts & amazing information

1

Maharashtra is India's largest cotton-area state and a leading sugarcane and soybean producer.

2

SRI (System of Rice Intensification) can save 30–40 % water while raising rice yields.

3

Sugarcane drip irrigation is mandatory on many cooperative sugar factory areas in Maharashtra to save water.

4

Chickpea variety ‘Phule Vikram’ is tall and upright, so it can be harvested by combine harvester.

5

Fall armyworm, a pest from the Americas, reached India's maize fields only in 2018.

Possible ESE questions

Short-answer questions

  1. What is CRI stage in wheat? (2 marks)

    Answer points: Crown root initiation stage, about 21 days after sowing – the most critical stage for irrigation.

  2. Name two adsali sugarcane varieties. (2 marks)

    Answer points: Co 86032, CoM 0265.

  3. Why is nipping done in chickpea? (2 marks)

    Answer points: To remove apical buds, encourage branching and more pods.

Long-answer questions

  1. Describe the package of practices for wheat cultivation. (10 marks)

    Answer points: Climate, soil, land preparation, varieties, sowing time, seed rate, treatment, spacing, fertilizer, irrigation at critical stages, weed, pests, harvest, yield.

  2. Give the production technology of sugarcane in Maharashtra. (10 marks)

    Answer points: Seasons, varieties, setts/bud chips, treatment, planting, nutrients, drip, earthing up, trash mulch, pests, harvest, ratoon.

  3. Compare the cultivation practices of soybean and chickpea. (10 marks)

    Answer points: Season, soil, sowing method, seed rate, Rhizobium, fertilizer, irrigation, pests (girdle beetle vs pod borer), harvest, yield.

  4. Explain the integrated management of pink bollworm in Bt cotton. (5 marks)

    Answer points: Refuge, timely sowing, short-duration hybrids, pheromone traps, rosette flower removal, crop termination, stubble destruction, chemical as last resort.

Key words

Package of practices
complete set of recommended practices for a crop
Puddling
ploughing flooded rice field to make soft mud and reduce percolation
Bud chip
single sugarcane bud with a small part of the internode used as seed
Refuge
non-Bt border rows that delay pest resistance
HDPS
high-density planting system in cotton
