The four crops that fill India's granaries and sugar mills

Rice and wheat together supply more than three-quarters of India's foodgrains, maize is the fastest-growing cereal for feed and industry, and sugarcane supports over 500 sugar mills and the national ethanol-blending programme. This lecture gives the complete package of practices for each crop under Maharashtra conditions.

17.1 Rice (Oryza sativa L.)

PracticeRecommendation
Climate and soilHot, humid; 20–35 °C; 1,000–2,000 mm rain or assured irrigation; clayey, water-retentive soils; pH 5.5–7.5 (Konkan, east Vidarbha)
VarietiesIndrayani, Phule Samruddhi, Ratnagiri-1, Ratnagiri-24, Karjat-3, Sahyadri hybrids, PKV HMT
Nursery1/10 ha raised bed per ha of field; seed 40–50 kg/ha; treat with carbendazim 2 g/kg; transplant 21–25-day seedlings
Land preparationPloughing, puddling to reduce percolation, levelling; bunds 15 cm high
Spacing20 × 15 cm, 2–3 seedlings per hill; SRI – 25 × 25 cm, single 8–12-day seedling
NutrientsFYM 10 t/ha; 100 : 50 : 50 kg N : P₂O₅ : K₂O/ha – N in three splits (40 % basal, 30 % tillering, 30 % panicle initiation); ZnSO₄ 25 kg/ha; urea briquettes deep-placed in Konkan
Water2–5 cm standing water; critical stages – tillering, panicle initiation, flowering; alternate wetting and drying saves 25–30 % water
WeedsPre-emergence pretilachlor or bensulfuron + pretilachlor; one hand weeding at 30 days
ProtectionBlast, bacterial leaf blight, sheath blight; stem borer, brown plant hopper, gall midge – resistant varieties, pheromone traps, need-based sprays
Harvest and yieldWhen 80–85 % grains turn golden; 50–60 q/ha grain

17.2 Wheat (Triticum aestivum L.)

PracticeRecommendation
Climate and soilCool growth (15–20 °C), warm dry ripening; medium to deep, well-drained black soils
VarietiesTimely: NIAW-917 (Tapovan), NIAW-1994 (Phule Samadhan), MACS-6222; late: NIAW-34, AKAW-4627; durum: MACS-3125
Sowing timeIrrigated timely 1–15 November; late up to 15 December
Seed rate and spacing100–125 kg/ha (late 125–150 kg); rows 22.5 cm (late 18 cm), depth 5 cm
Seed treatmentCarbendazim 2.5 g/kg + Azotobacter and PSB 25 g/kg
Nutrients120 : 60 : 40 kg/ha; half N + full P and K at sowing, half N at first irrigation (CRI)
IrrigationSix irrigations at CRI (20–25 DAS), tillering, jointing, flowering, milk and dough; with limited water give CRI and flowering first
WeedsMetsulfuron-methyl or 2,4-D for broadleaf weeds; clodinafop for Phalaris minor
ProtectionRusts (brown, black, yellow), loose smut, Karnal bunt; aphids, termites
Harvest and yieldWhen grains are hard and straw is golden; 45–50 q/ha

17.3 Maize (Zea mays L.)

PracticeRecommendation
Climate and soil21–30 °C; well-drained fertile loam; very sensitive to waterlogging
SeasonsKharif (June–July), rabi (Oct–Nov), summer (Jan–Feb)
VarietiesSingle-cross hybrids of public and private sector, Rajarshi, Manjari; quality protein maize (QPM); sweet corn and baby corn for markets
Seed rate and spacing18–20 kg/ha; 60 × 20 cm or 75 × 20 cm (≈ 66,000–83,000 plants/ha)
Nutrients120 : 60 : 40 kg/ha (hybrids 150 : 75 : 75); N in three splits – basal, knee-high, tasselling; ZnSO₄ 20 kg/ha
WaterCritical stages – knee-high, tasselling, silking and grain filling; avoid stress at silking
WeedsAtrazine 1 kg a.i./ha pre-emergence; earthing up at 30–35 days
ProtectionFall armyworm (since 2018) – early sowing, pheromone traps, sand + lime in whorls, emamectin/spinetoram as needed; stem borer; turcicum leaf blight
Harvest and yieldWhen husks dry and kernels show a black layer; 60–80 q/ha grain

17.4 Sugarcane (Saccharum officinarum L.)

PracticeRecommendation
Climate and soilLong warm growing season, cool dry ripening; deep medium-to-heavy, well-drained soils
Planting seasons (Maharashtra)Adsali July–August (18 months); pre-seasonal October–November (15 months); suru January–February (12 months)
VarietiesCo 86032, CoM 0265 (Phule 265), VSI 08005, Co 92005, MS 10001
Seed material9–10-month-old healthy cane; 25,000 three-bud setts or 10,000 bud-chip/single-bud seedlings per ha
Sett treatmentCarbendazim 0.1 % + Acetobacter and PSB; moist hot-air or hot-water treatment against grassy shoot and ratoon stunting
PlantingFurrows 100–150 cm apart or paired rows 75–150 cm; ridges and furrows or trench method
NutrientsFYM 20–25 t/ha; suru 250 : 115 : 115, adsali 400 : 170 : 170 kg/ha; N in 3–4 splits
WaterFormative (tillering) and grand-growth phases critical; drip irrigation saves 40–50 % water; trash mulching
InterculturalEarthing up at 4–5 months; propping; detrashing
ProtectionEarly shoot borer, top borer, woolly aphid, white grub; red rot, smut, grassy shoot
Harvest and yieldAt 18–20 °Brix (hand refractometer); 100–150 t/ha cane; ratoon with stubble shaving, gap filling and extra N

🧪 Try it in the field / lab

Measure the Brix of ten canes from the top, middle and bottom portions with a hand refractometer and decide whether the field is ready for harvest (top : bottom Brix ratio ≥ 0.95).

Facts & amazing information

Maharashtra and Uttar Pradesh together produce about two-thirds of India's sugar.

2

SRI rice can save 30–40 % water and 80–90 % seed.

3

India produces over 110 million tonnes of wheat – the second largest output in the world.

4

Fall armyworm, native to the Americas, was first detected in Indian maize in Karnataka in 2018.

Long-answer questions for ESE

Each question shows marks, Bloom's Taxonomy Level (BTL) and the course outcome it assesses.

  1. Formulate a complete package of practices for transplanted rice in the Konkan region and justify the nitrogen-split schedule. (10 marks · BTL-6 Create · CO2)

    Answer points: Varieties, nursery, puddling, spacing, FYM and NPK with splits, Zn, water, weeds, IPM, harvest; justification – N demand peaks at tillering and panicle initiation, losses in flooded soil.

  2. Analyse why crown root initiation is the most critical stage for irrigation in wheat and devise an irrigation schedule when only three irrigations are available. (10 marks · BTL-4 Analyse · CO2)

    Answer points: Physiology of crown roots and tillers; yield loss data; with three irrigations – CRI, jointing/tillering, flowering; reasons.

  3. Evaluate the integrated management of fall armyworm in maize. (10 marks · BTL-5 Evaluate · CO2)

    Answer points: Biology and damage; monitoring with pheromone traps; cultural, mechanical, biological (NPV, Metarhizium), chemical options; thresholds.

  4. Compare adsali, pre-seasonal and suru planting of sugarcane in terms of duration, yield, water use and economics. (10 marks · BTL-4 Analyse · CO2)

    Answer points: Planting time, duration, yield, sugar recovery, nutrient dose, water need, suitability.

  5. Explain sett treatment and bud-chip technology in sugarcane and assess their advantages. (5 marks · BTL-2 Understand · CO2)

    Answer points: Chemical, biological and heat treatments; bud-chip nursery; seed saving, uniform stand, disease-free crop.
