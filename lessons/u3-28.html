King of fruits, the giant herb and the tree of heaven

Mango is India's national fruit, banana is the world's most traded fruit and India its largest producer, and coconut – Kalpavriksha – gives food, oil, fibre and timber. These three crops support lakhs of farm families in Konkan, Jalgaon and the coastal belt.

28.1 Mango (Mangifera indica L.)

PracticeRecommendation
Climate and soilTropical; dry cool weather at flowering; frost-free; deep, well-drained laterite or alluvial soils, pH 5.5–7.5
VarietiesAlphonso (Hapus, GI-tagged), Kesar, Ratna, Sindhu (nearly stoneless), Keshar, Amrapali and Mallika (regular-bearing hybrids)
PropagationSoftwood grafting (Konkan) and stone/epicotyl grafting
Spacing10 × 10 m (100 plants/ha); high density 5 × 5 m (400 plants/ha)
Nutrients (bearing tree, 10 years+)50 kg FYM + 1.5 kg N, 0.5 kg P₂O₅, 1.0 kg K₂O per tree per year after the monsoon
Flowering regulationPaclobutrazol soil drench (≈ 0.75–1 g a.i. per m canopy diameter) in August–September for regular bearing
ProblemsMango hopper, fruit fly, stone weevil; powdery mildew, anthracnose; physiological – spongy tissue (Alphonso), alternate bearing, malformation
Harvest and yieldMature-green with shoulders raised and specific gravity > 1.0; harvest with stalk; ethylene ripening; 8–10 t/ha

28.2 Banana (Musa spp.)

PracticeRecommendation
Climate and soilWarm humid, 15–35 °C, no strong winds; deep rich well-drained loam; pH 6.5–7.5
VarietiesGrand Naine, Basrai (Dwarf Cavendish), Shrimanti, Ardhapuri; Rajeli and Safed Velchi for local markets
Planting materialTissue-culture plantlets (uniform, disease-free) or 3-month-old sword suckers
Seasons (Jalgaon)Mrig bag – June–July; Kande bag – October–November
Spacing1.5 × 1.5 m (4,444 plants/ha); paired-row planting with drip
Nutrients200 : 40 : 200 g N : P₂O₅ : K₂O per plant through weekly fertigation + 10 kg FYM per plant
OperationsDesuckering, earthing up, propping, removal of male bud, bunch covering with perforated polythene
ProblemsSigatoka leaf spot, Panama wilt (Fusarium TR4 threat), bunchy top virus; rhizome weevil, nematodes
Harvest and yield11–13 months after planting when fingers are ¾ round; 60–75 t/ha

28.3 Coconut (Cocos nucifera L.)

PracticeRecommendation
Climate and soilHumid tropical coast; 1,500–2,500 mm well-distributed rain; sandy loam, laterite and alluvial soils
VarietiesTall: West Coast Tall, Pratap, Banawali; dwarf: Chowghat Orange Dwarf (tender nut); hybrids T × D, D × T
Planting9–12-month-old seedlings with 5–6 leaves; pits 1 × 1 × 1 m with coconut husk layers; 7.5 × 7.5 m (175 palms/ha)
Nutrients (adult palm)50 kg FYM + 500 g N, 320 g P₂O₅, 1,200 g K₂O per palm per year in two splits
IrrigationDrip 40–60 L/palm/day in summer; basin mulching with husk
ProblemsRhinoceros beetle, red palm weevil, eriophyid mite; bud rot, stem bleeding
Harvest and yieldTalls begin bearing in 5–7 years, dwarfs in 3–4; 80–100 nuts/palm/year; harvest every 45–60 days

Long-answer questions for ESE

  1. Formulate a production plan for a high-density Alphonso mango orchard in Konkan and evaluate the role of paclobutrazol in regular bearing. (10 marks · BTL-6 Create · CO3)

    Answer points: Site, variety, softwood grafts, 5 × 5 m, canopy management, nutrition, PBZ dose and timing, IPM, harvest, post-harvest; evaluation of benefits and residue concerns.

  2. Analyse the causes and management of alternate bearing and spongy tissue in mango. (10 marks · BTL-4 Analyse · CO3)

    Answer points: C:N balance, hormonal causes, management; spongy tissue – heat and radiation at maturity, mulching, timely harvest.

  3. Design a fertigation-based cultivation schedule for tissue-culture banana in Jalgaon and justify each operation. (10 marks · BTL-6 Create · CO3)

    Answer points: Season, spacing, fertigation schedule by stage, desuckering, propping, bunch covering, IPM, harvest index.

  4. Describe the cultivation of coconut and evaluate the integrated management of rhinoceros beetle and red palm weevil. (10 marks · BTL-5 Evaluate · CO3)

    Answer points: Climate, varieties, planting, nutrition, irrigation; IPM – sanitation, pheromone traps, Metarhizium, crown cleaning.

  5. Explain why tissue-culture plants are preferred over suckers in banana. (5 marks · BTL-2 Understand · CO3)

    Answer points: Uniformity, disease-free, early and synchronised harvest, higher yield.

T × D
