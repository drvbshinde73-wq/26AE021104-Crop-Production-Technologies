Ten crops, ten success stories

These ten crops cover fruits (mango, banana, coconut), vegetables (chilli, tomato), flowers (rose, marigold), a spice (turmeric) and medicinal and aromatic plants (tulsi, lemongrass). Each is a proven income source for Maharashtra's farmers.

8.2 Fruit crops – mango, banana and coconut

  • Mango: tropical; deep well-drained laterite or alluvial soil; Konkan for Alphonso; flowering Dec–Feb, needs dry cool weather; NAA 20 ppm against fruit drop; harvest at mature-green (specific gravity > 1); ripen with ethylene.
  • Banana: warm humid climate; rich, well-drained soil; planting in June (mrig bag) or Oct (kande bag) in Jalgaon; drip fertigation; harvest 11–13 months after planting when fingers are ¾ round.
  • Coconut: coastal humid tropics; sandy-loam soils; pits 1 m³ with husk layers; irrigation in summer; bears 5–7 years after planting (talls).

8.3 Vegetables – chilli and tomato

  • Raise seedlings in protrays with cocopeat under insect-proof net; harden before transplanting.
  • Raised beds with drip and plastic mulch; stake tomato hybrids.
  • Integrated management of leaf curl virus: barrier crops (maize), yellow sticky traps, neem oil, roguing of infected plants.
  • Harvest tomato at breaker/pink stage for distant markets and chilli green or red-ripe for drying.

8.4 Flowers – rose and marigold

  • Rose: sunny site, well-drained soil; October pruning gives winter flush; polyhouse cut-rose production with bending technique; harvest cut stems at tight-bud stage, hold in water.
  • Marigold: hardy, grows in all seasons; pinching increases branches and flowers; staggered planting for Dasara–Diwali markets; African marigold petals give lutein for poultry feed and medicines.

8.5 Spice and medicinal & aromatic plants

  • Turmeric: planted May–June on raised beds; mulch with leaves; harvest after 8–9 months when leaves dry; cure by boiling 45–60 min, sun-drying and polishing; Sangli is a major market; curcumin 2–6 %.
  • Tulsi: hardy, low input; harvested at full bloom for leaves or distilled for essential oil (eugenol, methyl eugenol); used in Ayurveda.
  • Lemongrass: grows on poor soils and slopes; perennial for 5–6 years; steam distillation of fresh leaves gives oil rich in citral, used in perfumes, soaps and vitamin A synthesis.

Possible ESE questions

Short-answer questions

  1. What is spongy tissue in mango? (2 marks)

    Answer points: A physiological disorder of Alphonso where part of the pulp becomes white, sour and spongy.

  2. Why is pinching done in marigold? (2 marks)

    Answer points: To remove apical dominance and increase branches and flower number.

  3. What is the main constituent of lemongrass oil? (2 marks)

    Answer points: Citral (75–90 %).

Long-answer questions

  1. Describe the production technology of mango. (10 marks)

    Answer points: Climate, soil, varieties, propagation, planting, nutrition, irrigation, flowering regulation, pests/diseases, disorders, harvest, post-harvest, yield.

  2. Give the cultivation practices of banana with drip fertigation. (10 marks)

    Answer points: Climate, soil, tissue culture, planting season, spacing, fertigation schedule, desuckering, propping, bunch covering, pests, harvest.

  3. Explain the cultivation and curing of turmeric. (10 marks)

    Answer points: Soil, varieties, planting material, raised beds, nutrients, mulching, earthing up, harvest, boiling, drying, polishing, yield.

  4. Write short notes on cultivation of tulsi and lemongrass. (5 marks)

    Answer points: Species, climate, propagation, spacing, nutrients, harvesting, distillation, oil constituents, yields, uses.
