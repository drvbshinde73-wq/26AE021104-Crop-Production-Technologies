Many crops, many branches

A mango orchard, a tomato field, a rose polyhouse, a coconut plantation, a turmeric farm and a tulsi garden are all horticulture – but each needs its own knowledge. So horticulture is divided into branches, and its crops are classified on several bases to plan their production.

20.1 Branches of horticulture

BranchDeals withExamples
PomologyFruit crops (Latin pomum = fruit)Mango, banana, grape, pomegranate, citrus
OlericultureVegetable crops (oleris = pot-herb)Tomato, chilli, onion, okra, cabbage
FloricultureFlowers and ornamental plantsRose, marigold, gerbera, carnation, orchids
Landscape gardening / ornamental horticultureBeautifying surroundingsParks, lawns, home and institutional gardens
Plantation cropsPerennial crops grown on large estatesCoconut, arecanut, cashew, tea, coffee, rubber, cocoa
Spices and condimentsAromatic plant parts used for flavourTurmeric, ginger, pepper, cardamom, chilli
Medicinal and aromatic plants (MAP)Plants yielding drugs and essential oilsTulsi, ashwagandha, aloe, lemongrass, mint, vetiver
Post-harvest technologyHandling, storage, processing, value additionPulp, jam, dehydration, cold storage
Nursery management and plant propagationRaising quality planting materialGrafts, seedlings, tissue-culture plants

20.2 Classification of fruit crops

BasisGroups and examples
ClimateTropical – mango, banana, papaya, sapota; subtropical – citrus, pomegranate, guava, grape, litchi; temperate – apple, pear, peach, walnut; arid – ber, aonla, date palm
Life spanPerennial (mango), semi-perennial (banana), short-lived (papaya)
Growth habitTrees (mango), shrubs (pomegranate), vines (grape), herbs (banana, strawberry)
Fruit morphologyBerry (grape, tomato, banana), drupe (mango, ber, coconut), pome (apple, pear), hesperidium (citrus), syconus (fig), sorosis (jackfruit), balausta (pomegranate)
Ripening behaviourClimacteric – mango, banana, sapota, papaya, guava (ripen after harvest); non-climacteric – grape, citrus, pomegranate, litchi (ripen only on the plant)
Leaf fallEvergreen (mango, citrus) and deciduous (grape, fig, apple)

Explained simply

Climacteric fruits are like students who can finish their homework at home – they ripen after harvest. Non-climacteric fruits must ‘finish in class’ – they must be harvested fully ripe.

20.3 Classification of vegetable crops

BasisGroups and examples
Part usedLeafy (spinach, fenugreek), root (carrot, radish, beetroot), tuber (potato), bulb (onion, garlic), fruit (tomato, brinjal, okra), pod (beans, peas), flower (cauliflower, broccoli)
SeasonWarm-season (tomato, chilli, okra, cucurbits) and cool-season (cabbage, cauliflower, peas, carrot)
Botanical familySolanaceous (tomato, brinjal, chilli), cucurbits (cucumber, gourds, melons), cole crops – Brassicaceae (cabbage, cauliflower), Alliaceae (onion, garlic), legumes (beans, peas)
Hardiness to coldHardy (cabbage), semi-hardy (cauliflower), tender (tomato), very tender (cucurbits)
Method of cultureTransplanted (tomato, chilli, onion) and direct-sown (okra, cucurbits, root crops)

20.4 Classification of flowers, plantation crops, spices and MAP

GroupClasses and examples
FlowersLoose / traditional (marigold, jasmine, chrysanthemum, tuberose); cut flowers (rose, gerbera, carnation, gladiolus); pot plants; bulbous flowers; annuals and perennials
Plantation cropsBeverage (tea, coffee, cocoa); nut (coconut, arecanut, cashew); industrial (rubber, oil palm)
SpicesRhizome (turmeric, ginger), seed (coriander, cumin, fenugreek), fruit (chilli, pepper, cardamom), bark (cinnamon), flower bud (clove)
Medicinal plantsRoots (ashwagandha, shatavari), leaves (tulsi, aloe), seeds (isabgol), bark (arjun)
Aromatic plantsGrasses (lemongrass, palmarosa, citronella, vetiver), herbs (mint, basil), flowers (rose, jasmine)

Facts & amazing information

1

Tomato is botanically a fruit (berry) but is grown and used as a vegetable.

2

Banana is a giant herb, not a tree.

3

Coconut is called ‘Kalpavriksha’ – the tree of heaven – because every part is useful.

4

Climacteric fruits show a burst of respiration and ethylene production during ripening.

Long-answer questions for ESE

Each question shows marks, Bloom's Taxonomy Level (BTL) and the course outcome it assesses.

  1. Describe the branches of horticulture and evaluate their relative scope for employment generation in Maharashtra. (10 marks · BTL-5 Evaluate · CO3)

    Answer points: Nine branches with examples; employment – nursery, floriculture, processing, MAP; Maharashtra examples.

  2. Classify fruit crops on the basis of climate, fruit morphology and ripening behaviour, and analyse how ripening behaviour determines harvest and post-harvest strategy. (10 marks · BTL-4 Analyse · CO3)

    Answer points: Climate groups; berry, drupe, pome, hesperidium etc.; climacteric vs non-climacteric; harvest at mature-green vs ripe; ethylene ripening.

  3. Classify vegetable crops on different bases and apply this classification to plan a year-round vegetable production calendar. (10 marks · BTL-3 Apply · CO3)

    Answer points: Part used, season, family, hardiness, culture; calendar with warm- and cool-season crops; rotation by family.

  4. Distinguish plantation crops, spices, and medicinal and aromatic plants, and discuss their importance to the Indian economy. (5 marks · BTL-2 Understand · CO3)

    Answer points: Definitions; examples; exports; industries; essential oils; Ayurveda.
