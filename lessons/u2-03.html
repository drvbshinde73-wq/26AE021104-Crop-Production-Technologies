The right seed, at the right time, in the right way

Experienced farmers say: “A crop sown on time is half harvested.” The crop season decides which crop can grow, the sowing method decides how evenly plants stand, and seed treatment protects the seedling in its weakest stage. Together they decide the plant population – the foundation of yield.

3.1 Crop seasons of India

SeasonPeriodWeatherMajor crops
KharifJune/July – Sept/OctSouth-west monsoon; hot and humidRice, maize, soybean, cotton, tur, bajra, groundnut, kharif jowar
RabiOct/Nov – Feb/AprilCool growth, warm dry ripening; residual moisture or irrigationWheat, chickpea, rabi jowar, mustard, safflower, barley
ZaidFeb/March – May/JuneHot and dry; assured irrigationWatermelon, muskmelon, cucumber, summer groundnut, green gram

Maharashtra specials: sugarcane is planted in three seasons – adsali (July–Aug, 18 months), pre-seasonal (Oct–Nov, 15 months) and suru (Jan–Feb, 12 months). Onion has kharif, late kharif (rangda) and rabi crops; rabi jowar is sown on conserved moisture in September–October.

3.2 Factors deciding the time of sowing

  • Onset of monsoon and soil moisture – kharif sowing after 75–100 mm cumulative rain.
  • Temperature for germination and growth – wheat germinates best at 20–25 °C.
  • Photoperiod sensitivity of the variety.
  • Escape from pests and diseases – early sowing of cotton escapes pink bollworm peak; timely wheat sowing escapes terminal heat.
  • Previous crop, irrigation availability and market demand.

Key point

Late sowing of wheat after the first fortnight of November reduces yield by about 25–30 kg/ha for every day of delay because grain filling meets March heat.

3.4 Seed rate and plant population

Seed rate (kg/ha) = (Plant population per ha × 1000-seed weight in g) ÷ (Germination % × Purity % × 100). Plant population = 10,000 m² ÷ area per plant.

Worked example

Wheat at 22.5 cm rows with about 2.25 million plants/ha, 1000-seed weight 40 g, germination 90 % and purity 100 % → 2,250,000 × 40 ÷ (90 × 100 × 100) = 100 kg/ha.

3.5 Seed treatment

Seed treatment is the application of fungicides, insecticides, bio-agents, biofertilizers or nutrients to seed before sowing to protect it from seed- and soil-borne pests and pathogens and to improve germination and early vigour.

TypeMaterial and dose per kg seedPurpose
Chemical fungicideThiram 3 g, carbendazim 2 g, carboxin + thiram 2.5 gSeed rot, damping-off, smuts, wilt
Bio-agentTrichoderma viride/harzianum 4–10 gWilt and root rot of chickpea, soybean, cotton
BiofertilizersRhizobium, PSB, KSB 25 g (or 10 ml liquid)N fixation, P and K solubilisation in legumes
Azotobacter / Azospirillum25 gN fixation in cereals and millets
InsecticideImidacloprid or thiamethoxam as per labelTermites, sucking pests
PhysicalHot water 52 °C for 30 min; solar heat; moist hot air for sugarcane settsLoose smut of wheat, grassy shoot of sugarcane
Seed priming / hardeningSoak in water, KNO3 or CaCl2 and dry backFast, uniform emergence under dryland
Seed pelleting / coatingSeed coated with nutrients, lime or polymerBetter establishment in acid soils
Fungicide➜Insecticide➜Rhizobium / biofertilizer (last)➜Dry in shade➜Sow within 24 h

Key point

Follow the FIR order – Fungicide, Insecticide, then Rhizobium. Never mix biofertilizers with chemicals, and never dry treated seed in direct sun.

Facts & amazing information

1

Seed treatment costs less than ₹100 per hectare yet can prevent losses worth thousands of rupees.

2

A single sugarcane bud chip can produce a healthy seedling and saves about 80 % of seed cane.

3

Direct-seeded rice needs 80–100 kg seed/ha, whereas transplanted rice needs only about 40 kg.

4

Happy Seeder technology lets Punjab farmers sow wheat without burning rice straw.

Long-answer questions for ESE

Each question shows marks, Bloom's Taxonomy Level (BTL) and the course outcome it assesses.

  1. Describe the crop seasons of India and analyse how the time of sowing influences yield of rainfed and irrigated crops. (10 marks · BTL-4 Analyse · CO2)

    Answer points: Kharif, rabi, zaid; Maharashtra sugarcane and onion seasons; factors deciding sowing time; effects of early/late sowing – heat, pests, photoperiod; wheat example.

  2. Critically compare the methods of sowing of field crops and recommend a suitable method for soybean, wheat and rice with justification. (10 marks · BTL-5 Evaluate · CO2)

    Answer points: Broadcasting, dibbling, drilling, transplanting, vegetative planting, BBF, zero-till, DSR; merits and limits; recommendation – soybean BBF, wheat seed drill, rice transplanting/DSR.

  3. Calculate the seed rate of chickpea for a spacing of 30 × 10 cm when the 100-seed weight is 22 g, germination is 85 % and purity is 98 %, and explain each step. (5 marks · BTL-3 Apply · CO2)

    Answer points: Population = 10,000 ÷ 0.03 = 3,33,333 plants/ha; 1000-seed weight 220 g; seed rate = 3,33,333 × 220 ÷ (85 × 98 × 100) ≈ 88 kg/ha; steps explained.

  4. Explain the types of seed treatment and design a complete seed-treatment schedule for chickpea following the FIR principle. (10 marks · BTL-6 Create · CO2)

    Answer points: Chemical, biological, biofertilizer, physical, priming, pelleting; chickpea – thiram + carbendazim, then insecticide if termites, then Rhizobium + PSB + Trichoderma; doses; precautions.

  5. Evaluate conservation-oriented sowing methods such as zero tillage, BBF and DSR for sustainable crop production in India. (10 marks · BTL-5 Evaluate · CO2)

    Answer points: Principles, machinery, benefits – cost, water, residue, soil health; limitations – weeds, skills; suitable regions; evidence.
