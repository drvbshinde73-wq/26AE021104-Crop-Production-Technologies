Feeding the soil smartly

Indian farmers use far more urea than phosphate and potash – the ideal NPK ratio is about 4 : 2 : 1, but the actual ratio is often wider. Smart nutrient management fixes this imbalance using soil tests, the 4R principle and new products.

3.1 Soil Health Card (SHC)

  • Launched in 2015; every farm is tested for 12 parameters – N, P, K, S, Zn, Fe, Cu, Mn, B, pH, EC and organic carbon.
  • The card gives crop-wise fertilizer recommendations for that field.
  • Over 23 crore cards have been issued to farmers.

3.2 The 4R nutrient stewardship

RMeaningExample
Right sourceSuitable fertilizer formAmmonium sulphate for S-deficient soils
Right rateBased on soil test and yield targetSTCR equations
Right timeMatch crop demandSplit N in wheat at sowing and CRI
Right placeNear the rootsBand placement, fertigation, deep placement of urea briquettes

3.3 New-generation fertilizers

  • Nano urea and nano DAP (IFFCO) – liquid nano-particles sprayed on leaves; reduce granular fertilizer use.
  • Neem-coated urea – slows nitrification, mandatory in India since 2015.
  • Water-soluble and customised fertilizers for fertigation.
  • Biofertilizers and liquid consortia – Rhizobium, Azotobacter, PSB, KSB, mycorrhiza.
  • Integrated Nutrient Management (INM) – combine FYM, compost, green manure, biofertilizers and chemicals.

Facts & amazing information

1

Neem coating of urea reduced its illegal diversion to industries and improved N-use efficiency.

2

Only about 30–40 % of applied urea N is actually used by crops; the rest is lost.

3

One 500 ml bottle of nano urea is designed to replace about one 45 kg bag of urea for foliar top-dressing.
