Not one crop – a well-planned team of crops

Growing the same crop year after year tires the soil and invites pests and weeds. Indian farmers have long grown jowar with tur, soybean followed by wheat and cotton with green gram. These planned arrangements of crops in space and time are called cropping systems – the backbone of profitable and sustainable farming.

Explained simply

Mixed cropping is like guests sitting anywhere at a wedding; intercropping is like reserved seats – each crop has its own row and management.

16.3 Types of intercropping and their advantages

  • Additive series – a second crop added to the full population of the main crop (soybean + tur).
  • Replacement series – rows of one crop replaced by the other.
  • Parallel cropping – two crops with different growth habits and no competition (maize + green gram).
  • Companion cropping – crops that benefit each other.
  • Advantages: higher total yield, insurance against crop failure, better use of light, water and nutrients, legume N to cereal, weed and pest suppression, steady income.

Key point

Land Equivalent Ratio (LER) = (Yab/Yaa) + (Yba/Ybb), where Yab is the yield of crop a in the intercrop and Yaa its sole-crop yield. LER > 1 shows an intercropping advantage.

Worked example

Soybean sole = 25 q/ha, in intercrop = 20 q/ha; pigeonpea sole = 15 q/ha, in intercrop = 8 q/ha. LER = 20/25 + 8/15 = 0.80 + 0.53 = 1.33 → 33 % more land would be needed to get the same yields from sole crops.

16.5 Crop rotation – principles

Crop rotation is the growing of different crops in a planned recurring sequence on the same land over a period of time to obtain maximum profit with least damage to soil fertility.

  1. Deep-rooted crops follow shallow-rooted crops (cotton after wheat).
  2. Legumes follow non-legumes (chickpea after rice or sorghum).
  3. Exhaustive crops are followed by restorative crops (sugarcane → green manure → wheat).
  4. Crops of the same family and with common pests/diseases should not follow each other.
  5. Crops needing more tillage alternate with those needing less.
  6. Rotation suits soil, climate, irrigation, labour, market and the farmer's resources.
Soybean (kharif legume)➜Wheat (rabi cereal)➜Green gram (summer legume)➜Richer, healthier soil

Long-answer questions for ESE

  1. Differentiate mixed cropping, intercropping, relay cropping and sequential cropping, and analyse their relative advantages under rainfed conditions of Maharashtra. (10 marks · BTL-4 Analyse · CO2)

    Answer points: Definitions; row arrangement; management; examples; insurance, resource use, legume N; best choices for rainfed black soils.

  2. Explain the indices used to evaluate intercropping systems and calculate the LER for soybean + pigeonpea when sole and intercrop yields are 24 and 19 q/ha (soybean) and 14 and 9 q/ha (pigeonpea). (10 marks · BTL-3 Apply · CO2)

    Answer points: LER, CEY, ATER, RCC, aggressivity, MAI; LER = 19/24 + 9/14 = 0.79 + 0.64 = 1.43; interpretation.

  3. State the principles of crop rotation and design a three-year rotation for an irrigated medium-black soil farm that maintains soil fertility. (10 marks · BTL-6 Create · CO2)

    Answer points: Principles; example rotation with cereal, legume, oilseed, green manure; justification of each crop; benefits.

  4. Critically evaluate the sustainability of the rice–wheat cropping system and suggest diversification options. (10 marks · BTL-5 Evaluate · CO2)

    Answer points: Productivity plateau, groundwater depletion, residue burning, nutrient mining, weeds; options – DSR, zero-till wheat, legumes, maize, CA.

  5. Describe the concept of integrated farming systems and explain how they improve income and resource recycling for small farmers. (5 marks · BTL-2 Understand · CO2)

    Answer points: Components; synergy – residues, dung, manure; year-round income; risk reduction; examples.
