The smartphone in the farmer's pocket

Today a farmer in Satara can check mandi prices, get a weather alert, ask an AI chatbot about a leaf spot and sell produce online – all from a mobile phone. Digital agriculture is changing how farming decisions are made.

6.2 Artificial intelligence and data

  • AI apps identify pests and diseases from a leaf photograph.
  • Machine-learning models forecast yields, prices and pest outbreaks.
  • Satellite-based crop insurance assessment (PMFBY YES-TECH).
  • Blockchain-based traceability for export grapes and mangoes (e.g. GrapeNet, MangoNet).

6.3 Skills for the future agricultural engineer

  • Basics of data, GIS and sensors.
  • Understanding of crops and soils – the foundation of this course.
  • Entrepreneurship – agri-startups, FPOs and custom-hiring services.

Remember

Technology is the tool; agronomy and horticulture are the knowledge. An app can tell you a leaf is yellow – only crop science tells you why and what to do.

Possible ESE questions

Short-answer questions

  1. What is e-NAM? (2 marks)

    Answer points: National Agriculture Market – an online trading platform linking APMC mandis.

  2. Name one use of AI in crop protection. (2 marks)

    Answer points: Diagnosis of pests and diseases from leaf photographs.

Long-answer questions

  1. Explain the role of digital technologies in modern agriculture. (10 marks)

    Answer points: Platforms, AI, remote sensing, blockchain, advisory services, benefits and challenges.

  2. Write a note on Agristack and the Digital Agriculture Mission. (5 marks)

    Answer points: Farmer registry, crop survey, land records, uses for credit, insurance, advisories.
