The cheapest three decisions in farming

If seed is sown too deep, too thick or untreated, no amount of fertilizer later can rescue the crop. Method of sowing, seed rate and seed treatment cost little but decide the plant stand – and the plant stand decides the yield.

14.1 Methods of sowing

Sowing is placing seed in the soil at the proper depth and spacing so that it germinates and establishes a healthy plant stand. The method affects plant population, uniformity, weed control and the possibility of mechanisation.

MethodHow it is doneAdvantagesLimitations / crops
BroadcastingSeed scattered by hand over the field and covered by harrowingQuick, cheap, no implementUneven depth and spacing, high seed rate, weeding difficult; fodder, sesame, wheat in some areas
DibblingSeed placed in holes made with a dibbler or stick at fixed spacingLow seed rate, uniform standLabour-intensive; cotton, groundnut, castor, maize, vegetables
Drilling / line sowingSeed dropped continuously in rows by seed drill or seed-cum-fertilizer drillUniform depth, inter-cultivation possible, fertilizer placementNeeds implement; wheat, soybean, chickpea, jowar
Seeding behind the ploughSeed dropped by hand in the furrow opened by a country ploughBetter depth than broadcastingSlow; groundnut, chickpea in rainfed areas
PlantingPlacing vegetative parts (setts, tubers, slips) in the soilFaster establishmentSugarcane setts, potato tubers, turmeric rhizomes
TransplantingRaising seedlings in a nursery and planting them in the main fieldSaves main-field time, better weed control, uniform standLabour and water-intensive; rice, tobacco, vegetables, ragi
Mechanised / precision sowingPneumatic or inclined-plate planters, zero-till drills, Happy Seeder, paddy transplantersPrecise seed spacing, timeliness, saves seedCapital cost; maize, cotton, soybean, wheat after rice

Depth of sowing: as a rule of thumb, sow seed at a depth of 3–4 times its diameter – small seeds (sesame, mustard) 2–3 cm, medium seeds (wheat, jowar) 4–5 cm, large seeds (groundnut, maize, chickpea) 5–7 cm. Seeds sown too deep exhaust their food reserve before emerging; seeds sown too shallow dry out.

Watch out

A high seed rate does not guarantee a high plant stand. Broadcasting usually needs 25–30 % more seed than drilling, yet gives a less uniform stand, because some seeds fall on the surface, some too deep and some clump together.

14.2 Seed rate and plant population

The seed rate is the quantity of seed sown per hectare to obtain the desired (optimum) plant population. It depends on spacing, test weight (1000-seed weight), germination percentage, purity and expected field losses.

Formula: Seed rate (kg/ha) = (10,000 m² ÷ area per plant m²) × test weight (g) × 100 × 100 ÷ (germination % × purity % × 1000 × 1000) × plants per hill.

Worked example

Soybean at 45 cm × 5 cm, 1 plant per hill, test weight 120 g, germination 80 %, purity 98 %.

Plants/ha = 10,000 ÷ (0.45 × 0.05) = 4,44,444. Seed weight for 4,44,444 seeds = 4,44,444 × 120 ÷ 1000 g = 53.3 kg. Correcting for germination and purity: 53.3 ÷ (0.80 × 0.98) ≈ 68 kg/ha. The recommended rate of 65–75 kg/ha matches this calculation.

14.3 Seed treatment

Seed treatment is the application of fungicides, insecticides, bio-agents, bio-fertilizers or nutrients to seed before sowing to protect it from soil- and seed-borne pests and diseases and to improve germination and early vigour. It is the cheapest plant-protection measure – a few rupees per kilogram of seed.

TypePurposeExample
FungicidalControl seed- and soil-borne fungi (wilt, root rot, smut)Carbendazim 2 g/kg; thiram 3 g/kg; carboxin + thiram 2–3 g/kg
Bio-agentEco-friendly control of soil-borne pathogensTrichoderma viride/harzianum 4–10 g/kg
InsecticidalProtect from termites, shoot fly, white grub, sucking pestsImidacloprid 48 FS or thiamethoxam 30 FS as per label
Bio-fertilizerIncrease N fixation or P availabilityRhizobium (legumes), Azotobacter / Azospirillum (cereals), PSB – 25 g/kg or 250 g per 10 kg
Hardening / primingFaster, uniform germination and drought toleranceSoaking in water or dilute salts then drying (e.g. jowar in 2 % KH2PO4)
PhysicalKill internal pathogensHot-water treatment of sugarcane setts (50 °C, 2 h); solar heat for wheat loose smut
Order of treatment – FIR: Fungicide first → Insecticide next → Rhizobium / bio-fertilizer last, just before sowing. Chemicals can kill the living bacteria, so bio-fertilizer goes last and treated seed is dried in shade, not in sunlight.

In the field

Smallholders treat seed in an earthen pot or a seed-treating drum: seed and chemical are rotated for 10 minutes so every seed is coated. For Rhizobium, a slurry of 10 % jaggery solution is used as a sticker. Treated seed must never be fed to animals or people.

Try it yourself

Count 100 seeds of chickpea or soybean, place them between two layers of wet paper towel, roll it and keep it moist for 5–7 days. Count the healthy seedlings – that is your germination percentage. Now use it in the seed-rate formula to calculate how much seed you need for one hectare.

Facts & amazing information

1

Pneumatic precision planters place seeds within ±1 cm of the target spacing at speeds over 8 km/h.

2

Rhizobium seed inoculation can save 20–30 kg N/ha in pulses and soybean.

3

Kautilya's Arthashastra recommended dipping seeds in cow-dung water and honey – an early form of seed treatment.

4

In Punjab, the Happy Seeder sows wheat directly into rice stubble, reducing the need to burn crop residues.

5

Seeds sown deeper than about 3–4 times their own diameter may exhaust their stored food before reaching the surface.

Long-answer questions for ESE

  1. Describe different methods of sowing with their merits and demerits. (5 marks · BTL-2 Understand · CO2)

    Answer points: Broadcasting, dibbling, drilling, behind the plough, planting, transplanting, mechanised sowing; merits, demerits, crops.

  2. Explain seed treatment, its objectives and methods. (5 marks · BTL-2 Understand · CO2)

    Answer points: Definition; objectives; fungicidal, insecticidal, bio-agent, bio-fertilizer, priming, physical; FIR sequence; precautions.

  3. Derive the formula for seed rate and calculate the seed requirement of chickpea sown at 30 × 10 cm with test weight 220 g, germination 85 % and purity 98 %. Discuss the agronomic factors that justify deviation from the calculated value. (10 marks · BTL-3 Apply · CO2)

    Answer points: Plants/ha 3,33,333; seed 73.3 kg; corrected ≈ 88 kg/ha; deviations – late sowing, poor seedbed, rainfed, bold seed, pest losses, broadcasting.

  4. Critically examine how the choice of sowing method influences plant population, weed competition, water-use efficiency and mechanisation in field crops. Support with examples of rice and wheat. (10 marks · BTL-5 Evaluate · CO2)

    Answer points: Broadcast vs line vs transplanting vs DSR; uniformity, inter-cultivation, puddling and water, labour; zero-till, Happy Seeder, paddy transplanter; yield effects.

  5. Design a seed-treatment schedule for a soybean–chickpea sequence on Vertisols of Maharashtra, justifying each chemical and biological agent, the sequence of application and the precautions. (10 marks · BTL-6 Create · CO2)

    Answer points: Soybean – thiram + carbendazim, thiamethoxam, Bradyrhizobium japonicum + PSB; chickpea – Trichoderma for wilt, Rhizobium + PSB; FIR order; compatibility; shade drying; safety.

  6. Explain the rule for depth of sowing and give suitable depths for small, medium and large seeds. (5 marks · BTL-2 Understand · CO2)

    Answer points: 3–4 × seed diameter; sesame 2–3 cm, wheat 4–5 cm, groundnut/maize 5–7 cm; reasons.
