An orchard is planned for fifty years

A field crop lasts four months; a mango or coconut orchard lasts 50 years or more. A wrong site, poor layout or careless planting cannot be corrected later without heavy loss. That is why orchard establishment is planned like building a house – site, plan, layout and foundation.

3.1 Selection of site

FactorRequirement
ClimateSuited to the fruit: temperature, rainfall, humidity, frost and hot winds (e.g. Alphonso mango – Konkan coast; grape – dry winters of Nashik)
SoilDeep (≥ 1.5–2 m), well drained, medium texture, pH 6.5–7.5, free from hardpan, salinity and waterlogging
WaterAssured irrigation of good quality (low salts); drip feasibility
TopographyLevel or gentle slope; terraces and contour planting on hills
Market and transportGood roads, nearness to market, cold storage and processing units
OtherLabour, electricity, absence of pollution, history of soil-borne diseases (e.g. wilt in guava)

3.2 Planning the orchard

  • Fencing – live fence (Agave, Duranta, karonda, Jatropha) or barbed wire.
  • Windbreak – two to three rows of tall trees (casuarina, silver oak, eucalyptus, jamun) on the windward side; protects up to 10–15 times its height.
  • Roads, irrigation and drip lines, store, packing shed and nursery.
  • Tall trees on the north side, small trees on the south to avoid shading.
  • Evergreen and deciduous trees in separate blocks; pollinizer varieties planted where needed.
  • Intercrops and filler trees planned for early income.

Worked example

Mango at 10 × 10 m in the square system → 10,000 ÷ 100 = 100 plants/ha; in the hexagonal system → 100 × 1.155 ≈ 115 plants/ha.

3.4 Planting methods

  1. Mark the layout with a base line and right angles (3-4-5 rule) and fix pegs.
  2. Dig pits (1 × 1 × 1 m for mango; 60 cm³ for banana and guava) in summer; keep topsoil and subsoil separate.
  3. Expose pits to sun for 15 days to kill pests and pathogens.
  4. Fill with topsoil + 20–25 kg FYM + 1 kg single super phosphate + neem cake; add chlorpyriphos/neem against termites.
  5. Plant healthy, true-to-type grafts at the onset of monsoon (June–July) or in February–March where irrigated.
  6. Use a planting board so the plant stands exactly at the peg position; keep the graft union 15–20 cm above soil.
  7. Firm the soil, stake the plant, irrigate immediately and provide shade and a basin.

3.5 Care of young orchards and intercropping

  • Gap filling, staking, removal of rootstock sprouts, light training.
  • Regular irrigation, mulching of basins and protection from termites and sun-scald (whitewash trunks).
  • Intercrops for 3–5 years – vegetables, legumes, groundnut, papaya fillers; avoid tall and exhaustive crops close to trees.

🧪 Try it in the field / lab

Lay out a square and a hexagonal pattern for 6 m spacing on your college farm using a rope, pegs and the 3-4-5 rule, and count the plants in one acre.

Facts & amazing information

1

The hexagonal system accommodates about 15 % more trees than the square system.

2

Ultra-high-density mango orchards in Gujarat and Maharashtra plant over 600 trees per hectare.

3

A windbreak protects an area up to 10–15 times its own height on the leeward side.

4

Whitewashing young tree trunks protects them from sun-scald and borers.

Long-answer questions for ESE

Each question shows marks, Bloom's Taxonomy Level (BTL) and the course outcome it assesses.

  1. Evaluate the climatic, soil, water and market factors that should be considered while selecting a site for a commercial mango orchard in Konkan. (10 marks · BTL-5 Evaluate · CO3)

    Answer points: Climate, laterite soil, drainage, water, slope, transport, market and processing; justification for Konkan.

  2. Describe the systems of orchard layout with diagrams and calculate the number of plants per hectare for 8 × 8 m spacing in square and hexagonal systems. (10 marks · BTL-3 Apply · CO3)

    Answer points: Square, rectangle, quincunx, hexagonal, triangular, contour, HDP; 10,000/64 = 156; hexagonal 156 × 1.155 ≈ 180; diagrams.

  3. Explain the step-by-step method of planting a grafted fruit plant and analyse the reasons for failure of newly planted orchards. (10 marks · BTL-4 Analyse · CO3)

    Answer points: Layout, pit, filling mixture, planting board, graft union, staking, irrigation; failures – buried union, termites, waterlogging, poor plants, sun-scald.

  4. Design a plan for a 2-ha pomegranate orchard including fencing, windbreak, layout, irrigation and intercrops. (10 marks · BTL-6 Create · CO3)

    Answer points: Site plan; live fence; windbreak rows; 4.5 × 3 m rectangular layout; drip; roads and store; intercrops; diagram.

  5. Discuss high-density planting in fruit crops and assess its advantages and limitations. (5 marks · BTL-5 Evaluate · CO3)

    Answer points: Principle; dwarfing rootstocks, pruning, paclobutrazol; examples; early and high yield; high cost and skill; limitations.
