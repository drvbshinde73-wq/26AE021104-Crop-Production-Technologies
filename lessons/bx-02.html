Growing crops in a controlled home

When rain, hail, heat and insects keep spoiling crops, why not give the plant a house? Polyhouses, shade nets and soilless culture let farmers grow export-quality capsicum, cucumber and gerbera all year round.

2.1 Protected cultivation

Protected cultivation is growing crops in a modified, partly or fully controlled environment (light, temperature, humidity, CO2, water and nutrients) to protect them from weather and pests.

2.2 Soilless culture

  • Hydroponics – roots grow in nutrient solution (NFT, deep-water culture) – lettuce, herbs, strawberry.
  • Aeroponics – roots hang in air and are misted with nutrients – seed potato mini-tubers.
  • Substrate culture – cocopeat, perlite, rockwool in grow bags with drip fertigation.
  • Vertical farming – stacked layers with LED lights in cities.

2.3 Advantages and challenges

  • Yield 3–10 times higher than open field; year-round, off-season production; better quality and export.
  • Saves water (up to 90 % in hydroponics) and pesticides.
  • Challenges: high cost (subsidy under MIDH/NHB), technical knowledge, nematodes and salinity in soil-based polyhouses, market linkage.

Facts & amazing information

1

Polyhouse capsicum can yield 80–100 t/ha compared with 20–25 t/ha in open fields.

2

Hydroponic lettuce is ready in about 30–35 days after transplanting.

3

Pune and Nashik districts have one of the largest concentrations of polyhouses in India.

Possible ESE questions

Short-answer questions

  1. Define protected cultivation. (2 marks)

    Answer points: Growing crops in a modified controlled environment to protect them from weather and pests.

  2. What is hydroponics? (2 marks)

    Answer points: Growing plants without soil in a nutrient solution.

Long-answer questions

  1. Explain types of protected structures and their suitability. (5 marks)

    Answer points: Polyhouse, shade net, insect net, low tunnel; features and crops.

  2. Discuss soilless culture and its scope in India. (10 marks)

    Answer points: Hydroponics, aeroponics, substrate culture, vertical farming; advantages, limitations, crops, urban scope.
