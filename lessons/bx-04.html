Farming in a warming world

Unseasonal rain, heatwaves in March, longer dry spells – climate change is already in our fields. Climate-smart agriculture (CSA) aims to keep yields high, help farmers adapt and cut greenhouse-gas emissions – all together.

4.1 Three pillars of CSA

PillarAimPractices
ProductivitySustainably raise yields and incomesImproved varieties, INM, micro-irrigation
AdaptationBuild resilience to climate shocksDrought- and heat-tolerant varieties, contingency plans, crop insurance (PMFBY), weather advisories
MitigationReduce/remove greenhouse gasesAlternate wetting and drying (AWD) in rice, zero tillage, agroforestry, residue retention, biochar

4.2 Conservation agriculture

Minimum soil disturbance➜Permanent soil cover (residue/mulch)➜Crop diversification

Happy Seeder and zero-till drills allow wheat sowing into rice residue without burning – reducing air pollution and building soil carbon.

4.3 Carbon farming

  • Practices that store carbon in soil and trees – agroforestry, cover crops, compost, reduced tillage.
  • Farmers may earn carbon credits for verified carbon storage.
  • NICRA (National Innovations in Climate Resilient Agriculture) of ICAR demonstrates resilient practices in climate-vulnerable districts.

Facts & amazing information

1

Flooded rice fields emit methane; alternate wetting and drying can cut it by 30–50 % without yield loss.

2

Agriculture contributes roughly 14 % of India's greenhouse-gas emissions.

3

Stubble burning in north India releases millions of tonnes of CO2 and fine particles every year.
