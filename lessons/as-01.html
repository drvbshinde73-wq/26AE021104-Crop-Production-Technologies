Status of the Deccan Traps: one of the world's largest continental flood-basalt provinces, erupted about 66 million years ago at the Cretaceous–Palaeogene boundary; present extent about 5,00,000 km² with a cumulative lava pile up to about 2 km thick in the Western Ghats. Individual flows are 10–50 m thick. Basalt underlies roughly four-fifths of Maharashtra, which explains the dominance of smectite-rich black soils (Vertisols and vertic intergrades) in the state.

Why parent rock decides the soil

Because basalt contains 4–5 times more Fe, Ca and Mg than granite and almost no free quartz, it weathers to fine, base-rich, smectitic clay – black soil – whereas granite leaves quartz sand and kaolinite – light, red soil.

Q2. Differentiate between metals and non-metals.

Metals are elements that lose electrons easily to form positive ions (cations). Non-metals gain or share electrons and usually form negative ions (anions) or covalent compounds. In soil, nutrient cations (K⁺, Ca²⁺, Mg²⁺, Fe²⁺, Zn²⁺, Cu²⁺, Mn²⁺) come from metals, while N, P, S, B and Cl – non-metals – are taken up as anions.

BasisMetalsNon-metals
Physical stateSolids at room temperature (except mercury, liquid)Solids, liquids (bromine) or gases (O₂, N₂)
LustreShiny (metallic lustre)Dull (except iodine, graphite)
Malleability & ductilityCan be beaten into sheets and drawn into wiresBrittle; neither malleable nor ductile
ConductivityGood conductors of heat and electricityPoor conductors (except graphite)
SonoritySonorous – give a ringing soundNot sonorous
Density & melting pointGenerally highGenerally low
Electrons1–3 valence electrons; lose them → cations (electropositive)4–8 valence electrons; gain/share → anions (electronegative)
OxidesBasic oxides (CaO, MgO, K₂O) → make soil alkalineAcidic oxides (CO₂, SO₂, P₂O₅, NO₂) → form acids, acid rain
Reaction with acidsDisplace hydrogen from dilute acidsDo not displace hydrogen
Plant nutrients (examples)K, Ca, Mg, Fe, Mn, Zn, Cu, Mo, Ni, CoN, P, S, B, Cl (and C, H, O)
Uptake formCations: K⁺, Ca²⁺, Mg²⁺, Fe²⁺, Zn²⁺Anions: NO₃⁻, H₂PO₄⁻, SO₄²⁻, H₃BO₃, Cl⁻
ExamplesIron, copper, aluminium, zinc, gold, sodiumCarbon, nitrogen, oxygen, sulphur, phosphorus, chlorine

Metalloids (boron, silicon, arsenic) show properties of both groups – boron is an essential micronutrient and silicon is a beneficial element for rice and sugarcane.

✍️ Exam-writing tip

End with the agricultural link: metals supply nutrient cations held on soil colloids; non-metals supply anions that are easily leached (e.g. nitrate).

Low electronegativity (< 2) characterises metals that readily form cations; values above about 2.2 characterise non-metals that form anions or covalent oxy-anions. This is why metal nutrients are held on negatively charged soil colloids, while nitrate, sulphate and chloride move with soil water and are prone to leaching.

Q3. Differentiate between intrusive and extrusive rocks.

Igneous rocks are divided according to the place where magma cools. The cooling speed decides the size of crystals.

Other examplesPegmatite, syenite, dolerite (hypabyssal)Obsidian (glass), pumice (floats on water), scoria, tuff

Bowen's reaction series explains the mineral make-up of both groups: early, high-temperature minerals (olivine, pyroxene, Ca-plagioclase) dominate basic rocks such as basalt and gabbro; late, low-temperature minerals (K-feldspar, muscovite, quartz) dominate acid rocks such as granite and rhyolite. The same series predicts weathering order (Goldich, 1938) – the earliest-formed minerals weather fastest.

Explained simply

Imagine sand as footballs, silt as marbles and clay as grains of flour. Footballs leave big gaps (fast drainage); flour packs tightly and has a huge surface to hold water and nutrients.

Advanced data: Stokes' law and specific surface

Settling velocity of a particle in water: v = g d² (ρₛ − ρw) / 18 η, where g = 9.81 m s⁻², d = diameter, ρₛ = 2.65 Mg m⁻³, ρw = 1.0 Mg m⁻³ and η = 1.0 × 10⁻³ Pa s at 20 °C.

Particle (diameter)Settling velocityTime to fall 10 cmSpecific surface if spherical
Coarse sand (0.5 mm)≈ 0.22 m s⁻¹< 1 s≈ 0.005 m² g⁻¹
Fine sand limit (0.05 mm)≈ 2.2 × 10⁻³ m s⁻¹≈ 45 s≈ 0.045 m² g⁻¹
Clay limit (0.002 mm)≈ 3.6 × 10⁻⁶ m s⁻¹≈ 7.7 h≈ 1.1 m² g⁻¹ (plate-shaped clays: 10–800)

These calculations are the basis of the international pipette and Bouyoucos hydrometer methods of particle-size analysis. The very large measured surface of montmorillonite (600–800 m² g⁻¹) compared with the spherical estimate shows the importance of its thin, plate-like, expanding layers.

Remember

A rock is like a fruit salad; each mineral is one fruit in it. Granite = quartz + feldspar + mica.

(b) Minerals in detail

Definition: a mineral is a naturally occurring, homogeneous, inorganic solid with a definite chemical composition and an orderly internal atomic structure. About 5,000 minerals are known, but only about 20 are common rock-forming minerals.

Order of weathering (Goldich series): olivine > augite > hornblende > biotite > orthoclase > muscovite > quartz – minerals formed first at high temperature weather fastest; quartz is most resistant.

Average crustal abundance: feldspars ≈ 51 %, quartz ≈ 12 %, pyroxenes ≈ 11 %, micas ≈ 5 %, amphiboles ≈ 5 %, clay minerals ≈ 5 %, others ≈ 11 %. Elements: O 46.6 %, Si 27.7 %, Al 8.1 %, Fe 5.0 %, Ca 3.6 %, Na 2.8 %, K 2.6 %, Mg 2.1 %.

Lithosphere = crust + uppermost rigid mantle (≈ 100 km); asthenosphere = weak, plastic zone below (100–350 km) on which plates move.

(d) Layers of the atmosphere

The atmosphere is the envelope of gases held around the Earth by gravity, extending to about 10,000 km but with 99 % of its mass within 32 km. Composition of dry air: nitrogen 78.08 %, oxygen 20.95 %, argon 0.93 %, carbon dioxide ≈ 0.042 %, plus neon, helium, methane, ozone and variable water vapour (0–4 %).

Why it matters for crops

Agricultural importance: the troposphere supplies CO₂ for photosynthesis, O₂ for respiration, N₂ for biological fixation and rain for crops; the ozone layer protects plants and people from ultraviolet radiation.
