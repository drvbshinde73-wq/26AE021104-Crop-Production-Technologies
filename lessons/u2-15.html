The sky is the farmer's biggest partner

No farmer can control the sky – but a good agronomist plans around it. Temperature, rainfall, light, humidity and wind decide when a seed germinates, how fast a crop grows, when it flowers and how well grain fills. About two-thirds of the year-to-year change in Indian crop output is caused by weather.

15.1 Weather, climate and crop growth

Weather is the state of the atmosphere at a place and time (hours to days). Climate is the long-term (30-year) average of weather. Crop and variety selection is based on climate; daily operations such as sowing, irrigation and spraying are based on weather.

  • Growing degree days (GDD) = Σ[(Tmax + Tmin)/2 − Tbase] – heat units used to predict crop stages.
  • Vernalisation – cold treatment needed by winter wheat and some temperate crops to flower.
  • Thermoperiodism – response to day–night temperature difference (higher sugar accumulation in sugarcane with cool nights).
  • Injuries: frost (≤ 0 °C) in north India, chilling injury, heat stress and sun-scald.

15.3 Rainfall and soil moisture

  • India receives about 75 % of its annual rainfall from the south-west monsoon (June–September); average about 1,180 mm.
  • Rain decides sowing time of kharif crops, the success of rabi crops on conserved moisture and the need for irrigation.
  • Too little rain → drought, wilting, poor grain set; too much → waterlogging, nutrient loss, lodging and diseases.
  • Dry spells at critical stages (flowering and grain filling) cause the largest yield losses.
  • Effective rainfall – the part of rainfall used by the crop after runoff and deep percolation losses.

Key point

Contingency crop planning: when the monsoon is delayed, switch to short-duration crops and varieties (e.g. green gram, black gram, sunflower, early pigeonpea) and use higher seed rates.

15.4 Solar radiation and photoperiod

ComponentEffect on crops
IntensityDrives photosynthesis; cloudy weather at grain filling lowers rice and cotton yield
QualityRed and blue light used most (photosynthetically active radiation 400–700 nm, PAR); far-red affects stem elongation
Duration (photoperiod)Controls flowering: short-day crops – rice, soybean, jute; long-day – wheat, barley, mustard; day-neutral – maize hybrids, cotton, sunflower

Explained simply

Soybean is a ‘short-day’ plant: if you sow it very late, the shortening days make it flower while still small, so yield drops.

15.6 Climate change and crops

  • Rising temperature shortens crop duration and reduces grain filling (wheat yield falls about 4–6 % per 1 °C rise).
  • Erratic monsoon, more extreme rainfall events and longer dry spells.
  • New pest and disease patterns.
  • Adaptation – heat-tolerant varieties, adjusted sowing dates, micro-irrigation, mulching, crop insurance (PMFBY) and weather-based agro-advisories (IMD Gramin Krishi Mausam Sewa).

Facts & amazing information

1

A 1 °C rise during grain filling can reduce wheat yield by about 4–6 %.

2

IMD issues district-level agro-advisories twice a week.

3

The same rice variety sown on different dates may flower in nearly the same week because of photoperiod sensitivity.

4

One March hailstorm can destroy a year's grape crop in Nashik.

Long-answer questions for ESE

  1. Explain the concept of cardinal temperatures and analyse the effects of high and low temperature on growth, development and yield of wheat and rice. (10 marks · BTL-4 Analyse · CO2)

    Answer points: Minimum, optimum, maximum; GDD; vernalisation; heat stress at grain filling; spikelet sterility; frost; diagrams; management.

  2. Discuss the role of rainfall in Indian agriculture and formulate a contingency crop plan for a delayed monsoon in Marathwada. (10 marks · BTL-6 Create · CO2)

    Answer points: Monsoon contribution; effective rainfall; drought and excess; plan – short-duration crops and varieties, sowing windows, higher seed rate, moisture conservation, intercropping.

  3. Differentiate the effects of intensity, quality and duration of light on crops and apply the concept of photoperiodism to explain the effect of late sowing on soybean. (10 marks · BTL-3 Apply · CO2)

    Answer points: Intensity, PAR, photoperiod classes; critical day length; late sowing → early flowering, small plants, low yield.

  4. Evaluate the impacts of climate change on crop production in India and suggest adaptation and mitigation strategies. (10 marks · BTL-5 Evaluate · CO2)

    Answer points: Temperature, rainfall variability, extremes, CO2, pests; yield impacts; adaptation – varieties, sowing dates, water, insurance, advisories; mitigation – AWD, CA, agroforestry.

  5. Describe how humidity and wind influence crop growth, pest incidence and crop lodging, with suitable examples. (5 marks · BTL-2 Understand · CO2)

    Answer points: RH and diseases (blast, mildew), sucking pests; wind – pollination, ET, lodging, loo; windbreaks.
