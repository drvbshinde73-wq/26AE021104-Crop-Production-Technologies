1.1 What is soil?

Think of the Earth as a big ball with a hard rocky skin. On the very top of that skin, there is a thin, loose, crumbly layer in which plants grow. That loose layer is soil. It is the part of the Earth you walk on, dig in, and grow crops in.

Different books define soil in slightly different ways. All of the following are correct and you can use any of them in an exam:

  • Geological view: Soil is the outermost solid layer of the Earth, made by the breakdown (weathering) of rock.

  • Pedological view: Soil is a natural body formed from weathered rock by the action of climate and living organisms over a long time. It has layers (horizons) and keeps changing.

  • Edaphological (agricultural) view: Soil is a collection of natural bodies on the Earth’s surface made of mineral and organic matter, which is able to support plant growth.

In very simple words

Soil = broken rock + dead and decaying plant/animal material + air + water + living organisms, all mixed together in a natural way. It is “the foundation for life on land”, because almost all our food comes from it.

1.2 Components of soil (what is soil made of?)

A good soil is a mixture of four things. By volume, an ideal loam topsoil is about:

Component Approx. share by volume What it does
Mineral matter (sand, silt, clay, rock pieces) 45 % Skeleton of the soil; holds nutrients; gives texture
Water (soil solution) 25 % Dissolves nutrients; plants drink it from here
Air (soil air) 25 % Gives oxygen to roots and microbes; removes CO₂
Organic matter (OM) 5 % Food for microbes; builds structure; stores nutrients and water

Inside the 5 % organic matter, roughly 80 % is humus (the dark, stable, well-decomposed part), about 10 % is living roots and 10 % is soil organisms (earthworms, insects, bacteria, fungi).

Water and air share the pore space (the empty gaps). When it rains, water fills the gaps and pushes air out; when the soil dries, air returns. Water + air together are always about 50 % of the volume.

By weight, a dry soil is mostly mineral matter (about 95 %+) and organic matter is only a few percent – but that small amount matters enormously.

1.3 Chemical composition of soil

Soil is made of elements locked inside minerals and organic matter. Oxygen and silicon alone make up about three-quarters of the mineral part, because most soil minerals are silicates (silicon + oxygen compounds).

Element Typical % Where it lives in soil
Oxygen (O) 44 – 50 Oxides, silicates, water, organic matter
Silicon (Si) 20 – 33 Quartz, clay minerals (silicates)
Aluminium (Al) 7 – 15 Clay minerals, Al oxides
Iron (Fe) 3 – 6 Iron oxides and hydroxides (gives red/yellow colours)
Calcium (Ca) 1 – 5 Carbonates, silicates, exchangeable Ca²⁺
Potassium (K) 1 – 3 Feldspars, micas, exchangeable K⁺
Magnesium (Mg) 0.5 – 2 Silicates, carbonates, exchangeable Mg²⁺
Sodium (Na) 0.2 – 2 Feldspars, exchangeable Na⁺
Others (Ti, Mn, P …) < 1 each Various minerals

Composition of soil organic matter (the carbon-based part): Carbon 50–58 %, Oxygen 30–37 %, Hydrogen 5–6 %, Nitrogen 3–5 %, and small amounts of S, P and others (< 1 %). This is why we multiply organic carbon by about 1.72 to estimate organic matter (OC ÷ 0.58 = OM).

Soil is a natural mixture of four members: mineral matter, organic matter, water and air.

Mineral part = mostly oxygen + silicon + aluminium + iron. Organic part = mostly carbon + oxygen + hydrogen + nitrogen.

1.4 Soil science and its branches

Soil science is the study of soil as a natural resource on the Earth’s surface, including how it forms, how it is classified and mapped, and its physical, chemical and biological properties in relation to use and management.

Branch What it studies
Soil geology (mineralogy) The rocks and minerals from which soil comes and the process of formation
Soil physics Physical laws acting in soil – texture, structure, water movement, temperature
Soil chemistry Chemical components of soil and their reactions – pH, CEC, nutrients
Soil biology / microbiology Role of plants, animals and microbes in soil
Soil fertility and nutrient management How soil supplies nutrients and how fertilizers are used

The two main divisions of soil science:

  • Pedology (Greek pedon = soil): study of soil as a natural body – its formation (genesis), classification, morphology and description. The “origin” viewpoint.

  • Edaphology (Greek edaphos = ground): study of soil in relation to plant growth, nutrition and crop yield. The “plants” viewpoint.

Pedology = soil as a natural body; Edaphology = soil for plants. This pair is asked very often.
