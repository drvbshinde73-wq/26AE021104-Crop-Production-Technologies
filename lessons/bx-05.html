Back to nature – with science

Many consumers today pay more for food grown without synthetic chemicals. Organic and natural farming try to grow healthy food by feeding the soil life rather than the plant directly.

5.1 Organic farming

  • A production system that avoids synthetic fertilizers, pesticides, GMOs and growth regulators, relying on crop rotation, organic manures, biofertilizers and biological pest control.
  • Certification: NPOP (third-party, for export) and PGS-India (participatory, for domestic market); logos ‘India Organic’ and ‘Jaivik Bharat’.
  • Schemes: Paramparagat Krishi Vikas Yojana (PKVY); Sikkim became India's first fully organic state (2016).

The National Mission on Natural Farming (2024) promotes this low-cost system across India.

5.3 Strengths and concerns

  • Strengths: lower input cost, better soil health, premium price, safer food.
  • Concerns: yield dip in the first 2–3 years, certification costs, need for scientific validation and market linkage.

Possible ESE questions

Short-answer questions

  1. What is PGS-India? (2 marks)

    Answer points: Participatory Guarantee System – a farmer-group-based organic certification for the domestic market.

  2. What is Jeevamrut? (2 marks)

    Answer points: Fermented microbial culture of cow dung, urine, jaggery, pulse flour and soil used in natural farming.

Long-answer questions

  1. Differentiate organic farming and natural farming. (5 marks)

    Answer points: Inputs, certification, philosophy, cost, examples.

  2. Discuss the scope and challenges of organic farming in India. (10 marks)

    Answer points: Market, export, schemes, certification; yield, cost, inputs, awareness.
