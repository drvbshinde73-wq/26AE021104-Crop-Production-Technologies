Why every farmer is an agronomist

The word agronomy comes from the Greek agros (field) and nomos (management). Whenever a farmer decides what to sow, when, how much seed, how much water and fertilizer – he is practising agronomy. It is the science that turns soil, seed, water and sunlight into food for 1.4 billion Indians.

1.1 Definition of agronomy

Agronomy is the branch of agricultural science that deals with the principles and practices of field-crop production and soil management for obtaining maximum yield with minimum cost while keeping the soil productive.

  • Lyon: agronomy is the science of crop production and soil management.
  • Hughes & Henson: it is the branch of agriculture that deals with the theory and practice of field-crop production and soil management.
  • Modern view: an integrated science of soil, water, weather, crop and pest management for sustainable production.

Explained simply

Think of agronomy as the ‘management’ part of farming: soil science tells you what the soil is, plant breeding gives the seed, and agronomy decides how to grow that seed in that soil for the best harvest.

1.2 History of agronomy in India

PeriodMilestone
Indus Valley (≈ 2500 BC)Wheat, barley, cotton, sesame grown; evidence of ploughed fields at Kalibangan.
Vedic periodRigveda mentions ploughing, seed sowing and manuring; Krishi-Parashara describes weather forecasting and seed storage.
Kautilya's Arthashastra (≈ 300 BC)Crop seasons, irrigation charges and seed treatment with cow dung and honey.
1905Imperial Agricultural Research Institute at Pusa (Bihar); later moved to New Delhi (IARI).
1929Imperial (now Indian) Council of Agricultural Research – ICAR – established.
1960sGreen Revolution: HYV wheat and rice, fertilizers and irrigation; Dr M. S. Swaminathan and Dr Norman Borlaug.
1970s–90sDryland agronomy, cropping-systems research, AICRP projects.
2000 onwardsConservation agriculture, precision farming, Soil Health Card (2015), climate-resilient agriculture.

1.3 Scope of agronomy

  • Identifying suitable crops and varieties for each agro-climatic zone.
  • Choosing the right time and method of sowing, seed rate and plant population.
  • Soil management – tillage, conservation and fertility.
  • Nutrient management – balanced and integrated use of fertilizers, manures and biofertilizers.
  • Water management – irrigation scheduling, drainage and water-saving methods.
  • Weed management – cultural, mechanical, chemical and integrated methods.
  • Cropping systems – intercropping, crop rotation and farming systems.
  • Dryland and rainfed farming, contingency crop planning.
  • Harvesting, threshing and storage of field crops.
  • New frontiers – conservation agriculture, precision agronomy, climate-smart farming.

1.4 Importance of agronomy in India

  • Agriculture supports about 46 % of India's workforce and contributes about 18 % of GVA.
  • India must feed 1.4 billion people from only about 2.4 % of the world's land.
  • Good agronomy raised foodgrain output from about 51 million t (1950–51) to more than 330 million t today.
  • It raises yield per hectare, lowers cost and protects soil and water for future generations.
  • It links all other branches – soil science, plant breeding, entomology, pathology and agricultural engineering – on the farm.

Remember

An agronomist is like the captain of a cricket team – each player (science) has a skill, but the captain decides how to combine them to win (high, profitable yield).

Facts & amazing information

1

India is the world's largest producer of milk, pulses and jute, and second in rice, wheat, sugarcane, cotton and groundnut.

2

India's foodgrain production has grown more than six times since independence while the cultivated area grew only about 20 %.

3

‘Krishi-Parashara’, written about 1,500 years ago, gives rain-forecasting rules still quoted by farmers.

4

ICAR runs more than 100 research institutes and over 700 Krishi Vigyan Kendras (KVKs) across India.

5

The Green Revolution wheat variety ‘Kalyan Sona’ was released in 1967.

Possible ESE questions

Short-answer questions

  1. Define agronomy. (2 marks)

    Answer points: Science and art of field-crop production and soil management for maximum yield at minimum cost while maintaining soil productivity.

  2. Who is called the father of the Green Revolution in India? (2 marks)

    Answer points: Dr M. S. Swaminathan (globally, Dr Norman Borlaug).

  3. When was ICAR established? (2 marks)

    Answer points: 1929, as the Imperial Council of Agricultural Research.

Long-answer questions

  1. Define agronomy and explain its scope in Indian agriculture. (5 marks)

    Answer points: Definition with authorities; scope – crops and varieties, sowing, soil, nutrients, water, weeds, cropping systems, dryland farming, harvest, new frontiers.

  2. Trace the history and development of agronomy in India from ancient times to the present. (10 marks)

    Answer points: Indus valley, Vedic, Krishi-Parashara, Arthashastra; Pusa 1905, ICAR 1929; Green Revolution; dryland and AICRP; conservation and precision agriculture; Soil Health Card.

  3. “Agronomy is the mother branch of agriculture.” Justify this statement. (10 marks)

    Answer points: Integrates soil, plant, water, weather and pest sciences; decisions on the field; relation table; examples; contribution to food security.
