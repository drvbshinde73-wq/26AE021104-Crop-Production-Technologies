Colour on the plate and in the garland

Chilli and tomato are India's most important solanaceous vegetables and among the most price-sensitive crops; rose and marigold are the leading cut and loose flowers for markets, festivals and export. All four start in a nursery and need careful pest and virus management.

29.1 Raising healthy seedlings

  • Pro-trays (98 or 104 cells) filled with sterilised cocopeat + vermicompost; one seed per cell.
  • Raise seedlings under 40–50-mesh insect-proof net to exclude whiteflies and thrips that spread leaf-curl viruses.
  • Harden seedlings by reducing water for 5–7 days before transplanting.
  • Transplant 25–30-day tomato and 35–45-day chilli seedlings in the evening on raised beds with drip and mulch.

29.2 Chilli (Capsicum annuum L.)

PracticeRecommendation
Climate and soilWarm humid; 20–30 °C; well-drained black or loamy soils
VarietiesPhule Jyoti, Phule Mukta, Phule Sai, Pusa Jwala, Byadgi (colour), private hybrids
Spacing60 × 45 cm (paired rows on raised beds with drip)
NutrientsFYM 20 t/ha; 100 : 50 : 50 kg/ha (hybrids 150 : 75 : 75 through fertigation)
ProblemsLeaf curl complex (thrips, mites, whitefly), fruit rot and die-back, powdery mildew; silver-black mulch, barrier crop of maize, yellow and blue sticky traps
Harvest and yieldGreen chilli every 7–10 days; red chilli dried to 10 % moisture; green 150–200 q/ha, dry 15–25 q/ha

29.3 Tomato (Solanum lycopersicum L.)

PracticeRecommendation
Climate and soil18–27 °C; fruit set poor above 32 °C or below 15 °C; well-drained loam, pH 6–7
VarietiesPhule Raja, Bhagyashree, Dhanashree, Arka Rakshak (triple disease resistant), private hybrids
Spacing and stakingOpen-field 60 × 45 cm; staked hybrids 90 × 30 cm on wires 1.5–2 m high
NutrientsFYM 25 t/ha; 150 : 75 : 75 kg/ha (hybrids 250 : 150 : 150 through fertigation); boron and calcium sprays against cracking and blossom-end rot
ProblemsTomato leaf curl virus, early and late blight, bacterial wilt; fruit borer, Tuta absoluta leaf miner; marigold trap crop for fruit borer
Harvest and yieldBreaker stage for distant markets, red-ripe for local; 300–500 q/ha (hybrids higher)

29.4 Rose (Rosa spp.)

PracticeRecommendation
Climate and soilBright sunlight, 15–28 °C; well-drained loam rich in organic matter
TypesHybrid teas (cut flowers), floribundas, miniatures; Edward rose (Rosa damascena type) for gulkand and rose water
PropagationT-budding on Rosa indica var. odorata or R. multiflora rootstock
SpacingOpen field 60 × 60 cm; polyhouse 8–10 plants/m² on raised beds
PruningOctober pruning in Maharashtra for the winter flush; bending technique in polyhouses
ProblemsBlack spot, powdery mildew, die-back; thrips, mites, aphids
Harvest and yieldCut stems at tight-bud stage early morning and keep in water; 150–200 stems/m²/year in polyhouse

29.5 Marigold (Tagetes spp.)

PracticeRecommendation
TypesAfrican marigold (T. erecta) – large flowers (Pusa Narangi Gainda, Pusa Basanti Gainda); French marigold (T. patula) – small flowers, nematode-suppressive
SeasonsCan be grown year-round; plan transplanting so flowering coincides with Dasara–Diwali demand
SpacingAfrican 60 × 45 cm; French 40 × 30 cm
NutrientsFYM 15 t/ha; 100 : 50 : 50 kg/ha
PinchingRemove the apical bud 30–40 days after transplanting to increase branches and flowers
UsesLoose flowers for garlands; lutein (xanthophyll) for poultry feed and eye-health products; trap crop and nematode suppression
Harvest and yieldFully opened flowers picked every 3–4 days from 60–70 days; 15–20 t/ha

Long-answer questions for ESE

  1. Design a protected nursery and transplanting protocol for virus-free chilli and tomato seedlings. (10 marks · BTL-6 Create · CO3)

    Answer points: Pro-trays, media sterilisation, insect-proof net, seed treatment, hardening, transplanting, barrier crops, sticky traps.

  2. Analyse the causes of fruit cracking, blossom-end rot and poor fruit set in tomato and suggest remedies. (10 marks · BTL-4 Analyse · CO3)

    Answer points: Irregular irrigation, Ca and B deficiency, temperature extremes; drip, mulch, Ca/B sprays, varieties, timing.

  3. Evaluate staking and fertigation as technologies for increasing yield and quality of hybrid tomato. (10 marks · BTL-5 Evaluate · CO3)

    Answer points: Methods, plant population, disease reduction, fruit quality, fertigation schedule, economics.

  4. Compare the cultivation of rose for cut flowers in polyhouse with marigold for loose flowers in open field. (10 marks · BTL-4 Analyse · CO3)

    Answer points: Propagation, spacing, pruning vs pinching, nutrition, harvest stage, post-harvest, markets.

  5. Explain the importance of pinching and staggered planting in marigold. (5 marks · BTL-2 Understand · CO3)

    Answer points: Branching, flower number, market timing for festivals.
