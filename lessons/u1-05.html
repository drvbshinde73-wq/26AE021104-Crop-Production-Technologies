5.1 Definitions

A soil profile is a vertical section of soil through all its layers, from the surface down to the parent rock. Each distinct layer is a horizon; horizons differ in colour, texture, structure and composition. To study a soil, we dig a pit and look at the wall.

5.2 Master horizons

Horizon Nickname What you see Key facts
O Organic horizon Dark, soft surface layer of leaf litter and humus Leaf litter (undecomposed leaves, twigs, moss, lichens) on top; humus (decomposed) below. Some soils (peat) are only O layers. Intense biological activity. Typical thickness 2–5 cm.
A Topsoil / “biomantle” Top of the mineral soil; dark because of humus Most biologically productive layer; earthworms, fungi, bacteria live here; most roots; the layer farmers plough. Roughly 10–30 cm.
E Eluvial / leaching layer Light-coloured, mostly sand and silt, poor in clay Water leaches (washes out) clay, iron, aluminium and organic matter downward. Found mainly under forests; often absent in cultivated tropical soils.
B Subsoil / illuvial layer Red-brown or yellow-brown; firm, rich in clay Zone of accumulation of clay, Fe and Al oxides, carbonates, gypsum. Red-brown colour from iron oxides. Roots can reach here. Typically 30–100 cm.
C Regolith / parent material Large rocks or lumps of partially weathered bedrock Least affected by soil-forming processes; little or no organic matter; just weathered rock fragments.
R Bedrock Continuous solid rock mass Deepest horizon; colour of original rock (e.g., limestone, dolomite); may need blasting to dig.

5.3 Eluvial and illuvial zones

  • Eluvial zone (O, A, E): materials are washed out and carried downward. (Latin e = out.)

  • Illuvial zone (B): materials are washed in and accumulate – clay, carbonates, iron, gypsum.

Imagine pouring water through a tea strainer: the water takes small particles (clay, iron) out of the top and some settle in the lower layer. The top becomes pale (E) and the lower layer becomes rich and clayey (B).

5.4 Solum, saprolite and regolith

  • Regolith = all the weathered material from the surface to solid bedrock.

  • Solum = the upper, most strongly weathered part of the profile (O, A, E, B) – “the true soil” where roots and biological activity are concentrated.

  • Saprolite = the least weathered part of the regolith lying just above bedrock (largely the C horizon, still showing the rock’s original structure).

O A E B C R – remember “Old Aunts Eat Biscuits, Cakes and Rolls” (any phrase you like).

Solum = A + B (plus O and E if present). C is not part of the solum.
