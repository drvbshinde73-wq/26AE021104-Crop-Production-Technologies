From plan to planting

With the site chosen and the orchard planned, the trees must be arranged and planted correctly. The layout system decides how many trees fit in a hectare; the planting method decides whether they survive and grow fast.

22.1 Systems of layout

SystemArrangementPlants per haMerits / use
SquarePlants at four corners of a square10,000 ÷ (d × d)Simplest; cultivation in two directions
RectangularRow spacing wider than plant spacing10,000 ÷ (r × p)Room for machinery, intercrops and drip lines
Quincunx (diagonal)Square + one filler tree at the centre of each squareNearly double the square systemFillers (papaya, guava) give early income and are removed later
Hexagonal (equilateral triangle)Plants at corners of equilateral triangles, six around each tree≈ 15 % more than square (N × 1.155)Fertile valuable land; difficult layout
TriangularPlants of alternate rows midwaySlightly less than squareBetter light
ContourRows along contour linesVariesHilly and sloping land; soil and water conservation
High-density / ultra-high-density plantingClose spacing with dwarfing rootstocks, pruning and PGRs400 to over 1,600Mango 5 × 5 m or 3 × 2 m, guava 3 × 1.5 m, apple; early and higher yield

Worked example

Mango at 10 × 10 m in the square system → 10,000 ÷ 100 = 100 plants/ha; in the hexagonal system → 100 × 1.155 ≈ 115 plants/ha.

22.2 Planting methods

  1. Mark the layout with a base line and right angles (3-4-5 rule) and fix pegs.
  2. Dig pits (1 × 1 × 1 m for mango; 60 cm³ for banana and guava) in summer; keep topsoil and subsoil separate.
  3. Expose pits to sun for 15 days to kill pests and pathogens.
  4. Fill with topsoil + 20–25 kg FYM + 1 kg single super phosphate + neem cake; add chlorpyriphos/neem against termites.
  5. Plant healthy, true-to-type grafts at the onset of monsoon (June–July) or in February–March where irrigated.
  6. Use a planting board so the plant stands exactly at the peg position; keep the graft union 15–20 cm above soil.
  7. Firm the soil, stake the plant, irrigate immediately and provide shade and a basin.

22.3 Care of young orchards and intercropping

  • Gap filling, staking, removal of rootstock sprouts, light training.
  • Regular irrigation, mulching of basins and protection from termites and sun-scald (whitewash trunks).
  • Intercrops for 3–5 years – vegetables, legumes, groundnut, papaya fillers; avoid tall and exhaustive crops close to trees.

🧪 Try it in the field / lab

Lay out a square and a hexagonal pattern for 6 m spacing on your college farm using a rope, pegs and the 3-4-5 rule, and count the plants in one acre.

Facts & amazing information

1

The hexagonal system accommodates about 15 % more trees than the square system.

2

Ultra-high-density mango orchards in Gujarat and Maharashtra plant over 600 trees per hectare.

3

Whitewashing young tree trunks protects them from sun-scald and borers.

Long-answer questions for ESE

  1. Describe the systems of orchard layout with diagrams and calculate the number of plants per hectare for 8 × 8 m spacing in square and hexagonal systems. (10 marks · BTL-3 Apply · CO3)

    Answer points: Square, rectangle, quincunx, hexagonal, triangular, contour, HDP; 10,000/64 = 156; hexagonal 156 × 1.155 ≈ 180; diagrams.

  2. Explain the step-by-step method of planting a grafted fruit plant and analyse the reasons for failure of newly planted orchards. (10 marks · BTL-4 Analyse · CO3)

    Answer points: Layout, pit, filling mixture, planting board, graft union, staking, irrigation; failures – buried union, termites, waterlogging, poor plants, sun-scald.

  3. Discuss high-density planting in fruit crops and assess its advantages and limitations. (5 marks · BTL-5 Evaluate · CO3)

    Answer points: Principle; dwarfing rootstocks, pruning, paclobutrazol; examples; early and high yield; high cost and skill; limitations.
