Not one crop – a well-planned team of crops

Growing the same crop year after year tires the soil and invites pests. Indian farmers have long mixed and rotated crops – jowar with tur, soybean followed by wheat. These patterns are called cropping systems.

5.1 Definitions

TermMeaning
Cropping patternProportion of area under different crops at a time in a region
Cropping systemCropping pattern of a farm and its interaction with farm resources, technology and environment
Cropping intensity(Gross cropped area ÷ Net cropped area) × 100
Multiple croppingGrowing two or more crops on the same land in one year

5.2 Types of cropping systems

SystemDescriptionExample
MonocroppingSame crop every yearSugarcane–sugarcane, rice–rice
Sequential croppingTwo or more crops in sequence in a yearSoybean – wheat; rice – wheat
Relay croppingNext crop sown before harvest of standing cropRice – lathyrus (utera), cotton – wheat
Ratoon croppingCrop regrown from stubbles after harvestSugarcane ratoon, sorghum
Mixed croppingTwo or more crops mixed without definite rowsWheat + mustard + chickpea
IntercroppingTwo or more crops in definite row ratio at the same timeSoybean + tur (4:2), cotton + green gram
Strip croppingCrops in alternate wide strips across slopeBajra strips with groundnut
Alley croppingField crops between rows of trees/shrubsLeucaena hedgerows with sorghum

Explained simply

Mixed cropping is like guests sitting anywhere at a wedding; intercropping is like reserved seats – each crop has its own row.

5.3 Indices to evaluate intercropping

  • Land Equivalent Ratio (LER) = (Yield of A in intercrop / Yield of A sole) + (Yield of B in intercrop / Yield of B sole). LER > 1 means intercropping is advantageous.
  • Crop equivalent yield – yields of all crops converted to the main crop using prices.
  • Area–time equivalent ratio, monetary advantage and relative crowding coefficient.

5.4 Crop rotation – principles

  • Deep-rooted crops follow shallow-rooted crops and vice versa.
  • Legumes follow non-legumes (pulses add nitrogen).
  • Exhaustive crops (sugarcane, maize) followed by restorative crops (pulses, green manures).
  • Crops of the same family and with common pests should not follow each other.
  • Rotation should suit soil, climate, market, labour and the farmer's needs.
Soybean (kharif, legume)➜Wheat (rabi, cereal)➜Green gram (summer)➜Soil richer & healthier

5.5 Advantages of crop rotation and diversified systems

  • Maintains soil fertility and structure; balanced use of nutrients from different depths.
  • Breaks pest, disease and weed cycles.
  • Spreads labour and income through the year; reduces risk.
  • Improves water-use efficiency and resource use.
  • Integrated Farming Systems (crops + dairy + poultry + fishery) give steady income and recycle wastes.

Facts & amazing information

1

Rice–wheat is India's largest cropping system, on about 10 million ha in the Indo-Gangetic plains.

2

Sorghum + pigeonpea intercropping in Maharashtra often gives an LER of 1.3–1.5 – 30–50 % more than sole crops.

3

India's cropping intensity is about 155 %.

4

Including a legume in the rotation can save 40–60 kg N/ha for the next cereal.

Possible ESE questions

Short-answer questions

  1. Define cropping intensity. (2 marks)

    Answer points: (Gross cropped area / net cropped area) × 100.

  2. What is relay cropping? (2 marks)

    Answer points: Sowing the next crop in the standing crop before its harvest, e.g. utera cropping.

  3. What is LER? (2 marks)

    Answer points: Land equivalent ratio – relative land area needed under sole crops to give the same yield as the intercrop.

Long-answer questions

  1. Differentiate mixed cropping and intercropping. (5 marks)

    Answer points: Row arrangement, row ratio, management, harvesting, objectives, examples.

  2. What is crop rotation? Explain its principles and advantages. (10 marks)

    Answer points: Definition, principles (roots, legumes, exhaustive/restorative, pests, economics), example rotations, advantages.

  3. Describe different cropping systems with suitable examples and indices for evaluating them. (10 marks)

    Answer points: Mono, sequential, relay, ratoon, mixed, inter, strip, alley; LER, CEY, ATER; IFS.
