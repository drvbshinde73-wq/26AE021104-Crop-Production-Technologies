From the garden to the global market

Mangoes from Ratnagiri, grapes from Nashik, pomegranates from Solapur, bananas from Jalgaon – Maharashtra's fruits travel the world. Horticulture gives farmers higher income per acre than any field crop, and gives all of us vitamins, minerals and beauty.

1.1 Definition

The word comes from Latin hortus (garden, enclosure) and cultura (cultivation). Horticulture is the branch of agriculture that deals with the cultivation of garden crops – fruits, vegetables, flowers, plantation crops, spices, and medicinal and aromatic plants – and their processing and marketing.

Explained simply

Field crops are grown on large areas for grain; horticultural crops are grown intensively, with great care, mostly for fresh, high-value produce.

1.2 Importance of horticulture

  • Higher income per unit area – often 3–5 times that of cereals.
  • Nutritional security – vitamins, minerals, fibre and antioxidants.
  • Employment – labour-intensive operations, nurseries, processing and marketing.
  • Use of wastelands and hill slopes – fruit trees on marginal land.
  • Export earnings – grapes, mango, pomegranate, banana, onion, spices.
  • Agro-industries – processing, essential oils, floriculture, nurseries.
  • Environment – greenery, carbon sequestration, soil conservation, aesthetics.

1.4 Scope of horticulture in India

  • Diverse agro-climates allow almost every fruit, vegetable and flower to be grown somewhere in India.
  • Growing demand from urban consumers, processing and export.
  • Government support – MIDH (Mission for Integrated Development of Horticulture), NHB, PMKSY drip subsidy.
  • Protected cultivation, hi-tech nurseries and precision farming.
  • Post-harvest processing and cold chain – reducing 15–25 % losses.
  • Medicinal and aromatic plants, organic produce and agri-tourism.

Possible ESE questions

Short-answer questions

  1. Define horticulture. (2 marks)

    Answer points: Branch of agriculture dealing with cultivation of garden crops – fruits, vegetables, flowers, plantation, spices, medicinal and aromatic plants.

  2. Give the meaning of hortus and cultura. (2 marks)

    Answer points: Hortus – garden/enclosure; cultura – cultivation.

  3. Name two fruits in which India ranks first. (2 marks)

    Answer points: Banana, mango (also papaya).

Long-answer questions

  1. Explain the importance and scope of horticulture in India. (10 marks)

    Answer points: Income, nutrition, employment, wastelands, export, industry, environment; status figures; scope – climates, demand, schemes, protected cultivation, processing.

  2. Discuss the present status of horticulture in India and Maharashtra. (5 marks)

    Answer points: Production, area, world rank, leading crops; Maharashtra – grapes, pomegranate, banana, mango, onion; EGS-linked scheme.
