From the garden to the global market

Alphonso mangoes from Ratnagiri, grapes from Nashik, pomegranates from Solapur, bananas from Jalgaon and oranges from Nagpur travel across the world. Horticulture earns farmers more per acre than any field crop and gives all of us vitamins, minerals, colour and fragrance.

1.1 Meaning and definition

The word comes from the Latin hortus (garden, enclosure) and cultura (cultivation). Horticulture is the branch of agriculture that deals with the intensive cultivation of garden crops – fruits, vegetables, flowers and ornamentals, plantation crops, spices, and medicinal and aromatic plants – together with their propagation, protection, post-harvest handling, processing and marketing.

1.4 Scope of horticulture in India

  • Diverse agro-climates – tropical, subtropical, temperate and arid – allow almost every horticultural crop.
  • Rising demand from urban consumers, processing industries and exports.
  • Government support – MIDH, National Horticulture Board, PMKSY micro-irrigation, cluster development programme, Operation Greens (TOP – tomato, onion, potato).
  • Protected cultivation and hi-tech nurseries; tissue culture.
  • Post-harvest management and cold chain to reduce 5–15 % losses.
  • Organic produce, medicinal and aromatic plants, agri-tourism and urban gardening.

1.5 Problems and constraints

  • Small and fragmented holdings; shortage of quality planting material.
  • Pests, diseases and climate shocks (hail, unseasonal rain).
  • Post-harvest losses and lack of cold chain.
  • Price fluctuations and market intermediaries.
  • Low processing (only about 2–4 % of produce).

Long-answer questions for ESE

Each question shows marks, Bloom's Taxonomy Level (BTL) and the course outcome it assesses.

  1. Define horticulture and differentiate horticultural crops from field crops in terms of management, produce and economics. (10 marks · BTL-4 Analyse · CO3)

    Answer points: Hortus + cultura; definition; table of differences – scale, perishability, value, propagation, care, duration; examples.

  2. Critically examine the importance of horticulture for nutritional security, employment and export earnings in India. (10 marks · BTL-5 Evaluate · CO3)

    Answer points: Nutrition data; labour intensity; export crops; agro-industry; environment; supporting figures; limitations.

  3. Analyse the present status of horticulture in India and Maharashtra and identify its major constraints. (10 marks · BTL-4 Analyse · CO3)

    Answer points: Production, area, world rank, leading crops and states; Maharashtra crops; constraints – planting material, pests, post-harvest losses, markets, processing.

  4. Discuss the scope of horticulture in India and propose a development plan for a drought-prone district of Maharashtra. (10 marks · BTL-6 Create · CO3)

    Answer points: Scope points; plan – dryland fruits (pomegranate, custard apple, ber), drip, watershed, nurseries, FPOs, processing, schemes.
