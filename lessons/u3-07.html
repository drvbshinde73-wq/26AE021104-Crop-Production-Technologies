From the tree to the table without loss

India loses an estimated 5–15 % of its fruits and vegetables between harvest and the consumer. Harvesting at the right maturity, gentle handling, cooling and good packaging can save this loss, extend shelf life and fetch better prices – even in export markets.

7.1 Maturity and maturity indices

Physiological maturity is the stage when development is complete; horticultural (commercial) maturity is the stage at which produce has the qualities required by the market. A maturity index is a measurable sign of readiness for harvest.

IndexExample
Days from flowering / fruit setMango 110–120 days; banana 90–110 days after flowering

7.2 Ripening behaviour

Explained simply

Climacteric fruits (mango, banana, papaya, sapota, tomato) can be harvested mature-green and ripened later; non-climacteric fruits (grape, citrus, pomegranate, litchi, strawberry) must be harvested ripe.

Key point

Harvest in the cool hours of morning or evening, keep produce in shade, avoid dropping and remove field heat as soon as possible.

7.5 Causes of post-harvest losses and their control

CauseExamplesControl
PhysiologicalRespiration, transpiration, ripening, chilling injury, spongy tissue in AlphonsoPre-cooling, cold storage at correct temperature, coatings
MechanicalBruises, cuts during harvest and transportCareful harvesting, crates, cushioning
PathologicalAnthracnose, stem-end rot, Rhizopus, BotrytisField sprays, hot water, sanitation, cold chain
EntomologicalFruit fly, stored-product pestsBagging, traps, vapour-heat or irradiation for export
Infrastructure and marketLack of cold chain, long market chainsPack houses, FPOs, e-NAM, processing

Explained simply

A harvested fruit is still alive and breathing. Every operation after harvest aims to slow its breathing (cool it) and protect it from injury and germs.

Long-answer questions for ESE

Each question shows marks, Bloom's Taxonomy Level (BTL) and the course outcome it assesses.

  1. Define maturity and maturity indices, and evaluate the physical, chemical and physiological indices used for mango, banana, grape and pomegranate. (10 marks · BTL-5 Evaluate · CO3)

    Answer points: Physiological vs horticultural maturity; indices with examples; reliability; crop-wise recommendations.

  2. Differentiate climacteric and non-climacteric fruits with a respiration curve and analyse the implications for harvest, storage and ripening. (10 marks · BTL-4 Analyse · CO3)

    Answer points: Curve; examples; ethylene; harvest stage; ripening chambers; storage.

  3. Describe the post-harvest handling chain for export-quality grapes from harvest to the consumer. (10 marks · BTL-2 Understand · CO3)

    Answer points: Harvest, pre-cooling within hours, sorting, grading, packing with SO2 pads, cold storage at 0–2 °C, reefer containers, residue limits, traceability (GrapeNet).

  4. Analyse the causes of post-harvest losses of fruits and vegetables in India and propose an integrated strategy to reduce them. (10 marks · BTL-6 Create · CO3)

    Answer points: Physiological, mechanical, pathological, entomological, infrastructure causes; strategy – cold chain, crates, pack houses, FPOs, processing, ZECC, policy.

  5. Explain the principle and construction of a zero-energy cool chamber and its usefulness for small farmers. (5 marks · BTL-3 Apply · CO3)

    Answer points: Double brick wall with wet sand; evaporative cooling; temperature and humidity; shelf-life gains; low cost.
