From the tree to the table without loss

India loses an estimated 15–25 % of its fruits and vegetables between harvest and the consumer. Correct harvesting and post-harvest handling can save this huge loss and fetch better prices.

7.2 Harvesting methods

  • Hand picking with secateurs, leaving a stalk (mango 8–10 cm to avoid sap burn).
  • Harvesting poles with nets (mango harvesters) to avoid bruising.
  • Mechanical harvesting – tree shakers, combine-type harvesters for some vegetables.
  • Harvest in cool hours (morning/evening); keep produce in shade; avoid dropping.

7.4 Causes of post-harvest losses

  • High respiration and transpiration (water loss, shrivelling).
  • Mechanical injuries during harvesting and transport.
  • Microbial rots (anthracnose, stem-end rot, Rhizopus).
  • Physiological disorders (chilling injury, spongy tissue in Alphonso).
  • Lack of cold chain, poor packaging and long market chains.

Explained simply

A harvested fruit is still alive and breathing. Every operation after harvest aims to slow its breathing (cool it) and protect it from injury and germs.

Facts & amazing information

1

The zero-energy cool chamber developed at IARI keeps vegetables 10–15 °C cooler using only bricks, sand and water.

2

Mangoes exported to the USA and Japan must undergo irradiation or vapour-heat treatment against fruit flies.

3

Using plastic crates instead of gunny bags can cut transit losses of tomato by more than half.

4

Calcium carbide ripening is banned under the Food Safety and Standards Act because it can contain arsenic and phosphorus.

Possible ESE questions

Short-answer questions

  1. Define maturity index. (2 marks)

    Answer points: A measurable sign that indicates when produce is ready for harvest, e.g. TSS, colour, days from flowering.

  2. What is pre-cooling? (2 marks)

    Answer points: Rapid removal of field heat from harvested produce before storage or transport.

  3. Which gas is used for safe ripening of fruits? (2 marks)

    Answer points: Ethylene (or ethephon-generated ethylene).

Long-answer questions

  1. Describe maturity indices for fruits and vegetables. (5 marks)

    Answer points: Physical, chemical, physiological, computational indices with examples.

  2. Explain post-harvest handling operations of fruits. (10 marks)

    Answer points: Pre-cooling, cleaning, sorting, grading, treatments, waxing, ripening, packaging, storage, transport; examples.

  3. Discuss the causes of post-harvest losses and the measures to reduce them. (10 marks)

    Answer points: Biological, physical, physiological, handling and infrastructure causes; cold chain, packaging, treatment, processing, ZECC.
