Tiny amounts, huge effects

A spray of only a few milligrams per litre can make a mango tree flower every year, keep grape bunches loose with large berries, stop fruits from dropping or make a cutting root in two weeks. These powerful molecules are plant growth regulators (PGRs). Combined with special horticultural practices such as pinching, girdling and bahar treatment, they let the grower ‘steer’ the plant.

4.1 Plant hormones and plant growth regulators

Plant hormones (phytohormones) are organic substances produced naturally in one part of a plant and transported to another, where in very low concentrations they regulate growth and development. Plant growth regulators are natural or synthetic organic compounds that, applied in small amounts, promote, inhibit or modify physiological processes.

4.2 Auxins

  • Natural: indole-3-acetic acid (IAA). Synthetic: IBA, NAA, 2,4-D.
  • Rooting of cuttings – IBA 2,000–5,000 ppm quick dip (pomegranate, fig, grape, rose).
  • Prevention of pre-harvest fruit drop – NAA 20 ppm in mango and citrus; 2,4-D 10 ppm in citrus.
  • Fruit set and parthenocarpy – tomato (4-CPA).
  • Fruit thinning (NAA in apple), and herbicide action at high doses (2,4-D).

4.3 Gibberellins and cytokinins

PGRUses in horticulture
Gibberellic acid (GA3)Berry elongation and size in Thompson Seedless grape (dips of 10–40 ppm), bunch loosening; breaking seed dormancy (papaya, citrus); increase of fruit size; seedless fruits; delaying rind senescence in citrus; bolting and flowering in some vegetables
Cytokinins (kinetin, BA, CPPU, zeatin)Cell division; shoot formation in tissue culture; CPPU increases berry size in grape and kiwi; delay of leaf senescence; breaking apical dominance

4.4 Ethylene, abscisic acid and growth retardants

PGRUses
Ethylene / ethephon (ethrel, 2-chloroethylphosphonic acid)Uniform ripening of banana, mango, papaya (ethylene 100 ppm in ripening chambers); flower induction in pineapple; degreening of citrus; female flowers in cucurbits
Abscisic acid (ABA)Dormancy, stomatal closure and stress tolerance; colour development in grapes
Paclobutrazol (cultar)Gibberellin-biosynthesis inhibitor; soil drench (≈ 1 g a.i. per m of canopy diameter) induces regular flowering in mango and checks vigour
Cycocel (CCC), daminozide (B-9), maleic hydrazideDwarfing of pot plants, checking vegetative growth, preventing sprouting of onion and potato in storage

Key point

Calcium carbide (‘masala’) ripening is banned in India under the Food Safety and Standards Regulations; use ethylene gas or ethephon in approved ripening chambers.

Remember

Auxin = roots and fruit set · Gibberellin = grow longer and bigger · Cytokinin = divide cells · Ethylene = ripen · ABA = rest and stress.

🧪 Try it in the field / lab

Treat 20 pomegranate cuttings with IBA 2,000 ppm and keep 20 untreated. After four weeks compare rooting percentage and number of roots.

Facts & amazing information

1

Thompson Seedless grapes owe their long, large berries to GA3 dips.

2

Soil drench of paclobutrazol can make ‘off-year’ Alphonso trees flower regularly.

3

Ethylene is the only plant hormone that is a gas.

4

Pomegranate in Maharashtra is often given ‘hasta bahar’ (Sept–Oct flowering) to escape bacterial blight of the rainy season.

Long-answer questions for ESE

Each question shows marks, Bloom's Taxonomy Level (BTL) and the course outcome it assesses.

  1. Differentiate plant hormones and plant growth regulators, and classify PGRs with their physiological roles and horticultural applications. (10 marks · BTL-4 Analyse · CO3)

    Answer points: Definitions; auxins, gibberellins, cytokinins, ethylene, ABA, retardants; effects; uses with concentrations; diagram.

  2. Explain the role of paclobutrazol and ethephon in fruit production and evaluate their safe use. (10 marks · BTL-5 Evaluate · CO3)

    Answer points: Mode of action; doses; mango flowering, pineapple, banana ripening; residues; calcium carbide ban; safety.

  3. Describe special horticultural practices such as pinching, disbudding, girdling and bahar treatment, and apply them to improve flower and fruit production. (10 marks · BTL-3 Apply · CO3)

    Answer points: Definitions, procedure, purpose; examples – marigold, rose, grape, pomegranate; benefits.

  4. Design a bahar-treatment schedule for pomegranate to obtain fruits in the summer market while avoiding bacterial blight. (10 marks · BTL-6 Create · CO3)

    Answer points: Choice of bahar; rest period, defoliation with ethrel, pruning, fertilizer and irrigation schedule; disease escape; marketing.

  5. Analyse the effect of gibberellic acid on berry growth and quality of seedless grapes. (5 marks · BTL-4 Analyse · CO3)

    Answer points: Cell elongation; dipping stages and doses; bunch loosening; berry size, TSS; precautions.
