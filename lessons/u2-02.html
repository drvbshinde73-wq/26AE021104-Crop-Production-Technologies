Sorting crops like a librarian sorts books

India grows more than 100 field crops. To study, plan and manage them, agronomists group crops by their use, botany, season, life span and root system – just as a librarian groups books by subject. One crop can sit in several groups: maize is a cereal, a kharif crop, a C4 plant and an annual.

2.1 Why classify field crops?

  • To plan crop rotations and cropping systems.
  • To choose the right season and management.
  • To study crops with similar needs together.
  • To guide research, extension and policy.

Zaid / summerFeb–March → May–JuneWatermelon, cucumber, summer moong, fodder

Explained simply

Kharif crops like warm, wet weather; rabi crops need cool growth and warm, dry ripening.

2.4 Classification by life span, botany and photosynthesis

  • Life span: annual (wheat, rice), biennial (sugar beet), perennial (sugarcane ratoon, napier).
  • Botany: monocots (cereals, millets, sugarcane) vs dicots (pulses, oilseeds, cotton).
  • Family: Poaceae – cereals; Fabaceae – pulses, soybean, groundnut; Malvaceae – cotton; Brassicaceae – mustard.
  • Photosynthetic pathway: C3 – wheat, rice, pulses, cotton; C4 – maize, sorghum, sugarcane, bajra (higher efficiency in heat); CAM – pineapple, agave.
  • Photoperiod: short-day (rice, soybean), long-day (wheat, barley), day-neutral (maize, cotton hybrids).
  • Root system: shallow (rice, onion), medium (wheat, maize), deep (cotton, pigeonpea, sugarcane).
  • Water need: wetland (rice), irrigated dry (wheat), rainfed / dryland (bajra, jowar).

2.5 Special-purpose groups

  • Catch crops – short crops grown between two main crops (green gram, cowpea).
  • Cover crops – protect soil from erosion (cowpea, groundnut, horse gram).
  • Trap crops – attract pests away from the main crop (marigold in tomato, African tall marigold against nematodes).
  • Smother crops – suppress weeds by dense growth (cowpea, horse gram).
  • Cash crops – grown mainly for sale (cotton, sugarcane, tobacco).

Maize is called the ‘queen of cereals’ because of its very high genetic yield potential.

2

Possible ESE questions

Short-answer questions

  1. What are kharif crops? Give two examples. (2 marks)

    Answer points: Crops sown at the onset of the monsoon (June–July) and harvested in Sept–Oct; rice, cotton.

  2. Define catch crop. (2 marks)

    Answer points: A short-duration crop grown between two main crops to use the idle land and resources.

  3. Name two C4 field crops. (2 marks)

    Answer points: Maize, sugarcane (also sorghum, bajra).

Long-answer questions

  1. Classify field crops according to economic use with examples. (5 marks)

    Answer points: Cereals, millets, pulses, oilseeds, fibre, sugar, tuber, fodder, narcotics, green manure, spices – examples each.

  2. Give an account of the various bases of classification of field crops. (10 marks)

    Answer points: Economic use, season, life span, botany/family, photosynthetic pathway, photoperiod, root depth, water need, special-purpose crops; examples and table.

  3. Differentiate C3 and C4 crops and explain their agronomic significance. (5 marks)

    Answer points: First stable product (3-PGA vs OAA), Kranz anatomy, photorespiration, water-use efficiency, temperature optimum, examples, implications for heat and drought.
