Farming with eyes in the sky

Drones that spray an acre in seven minutes, sensors that text the farmer when the soil is dry, and tractors that steer themselves by GPS – this is precision agriculture: doing the right thing, at the right place, at the right time, in the right amount.

1.1 What is precision agriculture?

Precision agriculture is a farm-management approach that uses information technology – GPS, GIS, sensors, drones and data analytics – to observe, measure and respond to variability within a field.

Collect data (sensors, drones, satellites)➜Analyse (GIS, AI)➜Decide➜Variable-rate action➜Evaluate yield map

1.2 Tools of precision agriculture

ToolUse on the farm
GPS / GNSSLocate every point; auto-steer tractors; mark soil samples
GISMake maps of soil, yield and pests
Remote sensing & NDVISatellite/drone images show crop health; NDVI = (NIR − Red)/(NIR + Red)
Drones (UAVs)Spraying, mapping, crop counting; Kisan Drone scheme (2022)
Soil and weather sensors (IoT)Moisture, temperature, EC sent to mobile phone
Variable-rate technology (VRT)Seed, fertilizer and pesticide applied at different rates in different zones
Yield monitorsCombine harvesters record yield every few metres

1.3 Benefits and limitations

  • Saves 10–30 % fertilizer, water and pesticides; reduces pollution.
  • Higher yield and quality; better records and traceability.
  • Limitations: high cost, small and scattered holdings, need for skills and connectivity – solved by custom-hiring centres and FPOs.

Explained simply

Instead of treating the whole field as one patient, precision farming examines each square metre like a doctor and gives only the medicine that spot needs.

Facts & amazing information

1

A drone can spray one acre in about 7–10 minutes using only about 10 litres of liquid.

2

India permits drone spraying of approved pesticides under SOPs issued in 2021–22.

3

The NDVI of healthy green crops is usually above 0.6.

Long-answer questions for ESE

  1. Explain the components of precision agriculture and evaluate their applicability to small and fragmented farms in India. (10 marks · BTL-5 Evaluate · CO4)

    Answer points: GPS, GIS, remote sensing, sensors, VRT, drones, yield monitors; constraints; custom hiring, FPOs.

  2. Analyse how NDVI derived from satellite or drone imagery can guide nitrogen and irrigation management. (10 marks · BTL-4 Analyse · CO4)

    Answer points: NDVI formula; interpretation; zone maps; VRT; examples.

  3. Design a drone-based crop-monitoring and spraying programme for a 50-ha soybean cluster, considering regulations and costs. (10 marks · BTL-6 Create · CO4)

    Answer points: Mapping, scouting, spraying, SOPs, licensing, cost–benefit, safety.
